On 19 November 1969, Pelé scored from the penalty spot for Santos against Vasco da Gama at the Maracanã.

It was the 1,000th goal of his career. It also won the game: Santos beat Vasco 2–1.

On 19 November 2005, Ronaldinho scored twice as Barcelona won 3–0 at the Bernabéu.

And the Real Madrid crowd stood and applauded him.

Same date. Two Brazilians. 36 years apart. Two very different kinds of glory.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Pelé's thousandth goal was not a dribble or a volley. It was a penalty, watched by a crowd of more than 65,000.

The milestone came from the plainest chance in football.

He had reached the number partly through days like one in 1964, when he scored eight goals in a single match against Botafogo.

Ronaldinho's night was measured in a different way: not by a count, but by a reaction.

Samuel Eto'o opened the scoring. Ronaldinho added the second and the third.

The standing ovation came from Real Madrid's supporters, in their own stadium, for the player who had just scored twice against their team.

That year he won the Ballon d'Or.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Pelé never won the Ballon d'Or.

In 1969 it was open only to Europeans. A player from outside Europe could not win it until 1995, when George Weah, from Liberia, became the first.

So in 1969, the man with a thousand goals was not even eligible for the prize Ronaldinho collected in the year of his ovation.

Ronaldinho's own story at Barcelona did not run much longer. In 2008, less than three years after that night in Madrid, he left for Milan.

Here's what stays with me.

One night is remembered as a number. The other is remembered as a sound.

A thousand goals takes a whole career. A rival crowd on its feet takes one evening.

Football found a way to honour both of them.
