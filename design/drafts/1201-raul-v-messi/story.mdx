On 1 December 1998, Real Madrid and Vasco da Gama, the champions of South America, were level at 1–1 in Tokyo going into the last ten minutes of the Intercontinental Cup. Juninho Pernambucano had equalised for Vasco.

Clarence Seedorf sent a long ball forward. Raúl, 21, brought it down, left two defenders with a dummy and scored with his weaker right foot.

Real Madrid won 2–1, their first Intercontinental Cup since 1960. Raúl was named man of the match.

On 1 December 2009, in Paris, Lionel Messi won his first Ballon d'Or. He was 22.

Two Liga legends. Same date. Eleven years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Raúl never won the Ballon d'Or.

He came closest in 2001, the year he finished as La Liga's top scorer for the second time. Michael Owen won with 176 points. Raúl was second with 140.

Messi's first one was a landslide. He took 473 of a possible 480 points. Cristiano Ronaldo, in second place, had 233. Xavi, Messi's Barcelona team-mate, was third.

No winner had ever finished that far ahead of the runner-up.

That May, in Rome, Messi had scored Barcelona's second goal in the Champions League final against Manchester United. Xavi crossed. The smallest man on the pitch headed it in.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Raúl scored 323 goals for Real Madrid, a club record when he left in 2010.

It stood for five years after he left. In October 2015 it was broken by Cristiano Ronaldo, the same man who had finished second on Messi's night in Paris.

Messi went on to win the award eight times.

Here's what stays with me.

Raúl's night in Tokyo ended with a trophy and a goal the club still marks every 1 December. No Ballon d'Or ever followed.

Messi's first Ballon d'Or was the start of eight.

One won a world club final at 21. The other was voted the world's best at 22.
