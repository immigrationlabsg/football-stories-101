On 7 November 2010, Fernando Torres scored twice at Anfield, in the 11th and the 44th minutes. Liverpool beat Chelsea 2–0.

On 7 November 2012, Celtic beat Barcelona 2–1 at Celtic Park in the Champions League. Their second goal, the one that won it, came from Tony Watt, an 18-year-old substitute.

Same date. Two giants beaten. One by a striker every defender knew, the other by a teenager off the bench.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Torres's first came from a Dirk Kuyt pass. The second was a curling shot after Raul Meireles found him.

Torres had done this to Chelsea before. That double took him to seven goals in eight games against them for Liverpool.

Chelsea arrived at Anfield as league leaders. They went home with only their second defeat of the season.

Celtic's night was even less likely. Victor Wanyama headed them in front from a corner after 21 minutes.

After that, it was Barcelona against Fraser Forster, in Celtic's goal. He kept saving.

Late in the second half, Watt ran on to a long clearance, got clear of the defence and made it 2–0. Lionel Messi pulled one back in stoppage time. It was too late.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Twelve weeks after his double, Torres joined Chelsea, the team he had just beaten, for a British record £50 million.

Watt's goal stayed exactly what it was: the winner against Barcelona, on a night when Messi scored and still lost.

Two nights against giants on the same date. One was followed, within weeks, by a record transfer. The other was complete the moment the final whistle blew at Celtic Park.

Here's what stays with me.

A giant can be brought down by a famous name or by a teenager nobody had planned around.

Torres's goals that day are now remembered alongside the shirt he wore next.

Watt's goal is remembered on its own, and that is enough.
