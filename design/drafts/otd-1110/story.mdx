On 10 November 2013, Robin van Persie headed the only goal at Old Trafford. Manchester United beat Arsenal, his old club, 1–0.

On 10 November 2019, Mohamed Salah headed Liverpool's second goal at Anfield. Liverpool beat Manchester City 3–1.

Same date, six years apart. Two title races, and two goals scored with the head.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The two games were mirror images.

In 2013, Van Persie was on the champions' side. He had left Arsenal for United in 2012, and in his first season United won the league with him as the Premier League's top scorer.

He had been the top scorer at Arsenal the season before that, too.

Arsenal came to Old Trafford top of the table. Van Persie met a Wayne Rooney corner after 27 minutes and headed it in.

In 2019 it was the other way round. Manchester City were the champions. Liverpool were top, and City came to Anfield to close the gap.

Fabinho scored first, with a long-range shot after six minutes. Seven minutes later, Salah met an Andy Robertson cross and headed in the second. Sadio Mané added a third after half-time. Bernardo Silva's late goal for City changed nothing.

So one man beat the leaders for the champions. The other beat the champions for the leaders.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The two wins led to very different places.

That night, Arsenal were still top. United finished the season seventh.

Liverpool left the pitch nine points clear of City. They went on to win the league, their first title in 30 years.

Same date. Same kind of goal. One season fell away, and the other ended with the trophy.

Here's what stays with me.

A November header does not decide a season.

What comes after it does.

One of these goals is a bright night in a hard year. The other is a step on the way to a title.
