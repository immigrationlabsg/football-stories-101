On 4 November 1992, Eric Cantona scored late for Leeds United against Rangers at Elland Road, in a European Cup tie billed as the Battle of Britain. It was not enough. Leeds lost 2–1 on the night and went out, 4–2 on aggregate.

On 4 November 2001, Michael Owen scored twice for Liverpool against Manchester United at Anfield, either side of a John Arne Riise free kick. Liverpool won 3–1. David Beckham scored United's goal.

Same date. Nine years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Both men ended up at Manchester United.

Cantona went first, and fast. Three weeks after the Rangers game, Leeds sold him to United.

He had joined Leeds only that February, and won the league with them that spring. In his first season at Old Trafford he won it again: back-to-back titles with two different clubs.

He won 4 league titles in 5 seasons at United, then retired in May 1997, aged 30.

Owen took the long way round.

In 2001 he scored both Liverpool goals in the FA Cup final. Liverpool also won the League Cup and the UEFA Cup that season.

That December he won the Ballon d'Or, the first Englishman to win it since Kevin Keegan in 1979.

Then came Real Madrid in 2004, where he scored 13 league goals in one season, and Newcastle a year later.

When Newcastle were relegated in 2009 and his contract ran out, he joined Manchester United on a free transfer. Headlines called it a shock.

Then came the part that surprised me.

For all those goals at Liverpool, Owen never won the league there.

The only league title of his career came with Manchester United, in 2011.

The striker who scored twice against them at Anfield won his one league medal in their colours.

Here's what stays with me.

Rivalries feel permanent. Careers rarely are.

Cantona arrives at United, and United become champions. Owen arrives, and he becomes one.
