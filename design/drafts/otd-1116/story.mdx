On 16 November 2002, Thierry Henry picked up the ball in his own half against Tottenham at Highbury.

He ran at the Spurs defence and finished with his left foot past Kasey Keller. Then he sprinted the length of the pitch and slid on his knees in front of the Tottenham fans. Arsenal won 3–0.

On 16 November 1988, Roberto Baggio played for Italy for the first time. He was 21. Italy beat the Netherlands, the European champions, 1–0.

Same date. Two forwards. 14 years apart. One in full flight, one just starting out.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Both men played for Juventus. And Juventus let both of them go.

Baggio arrived from Fiorentina in 1990 for a world-record fee. Fiorentina's fans took to the streets of Florence in protest.

At Juventus he won the Ballon d'Or in 1993. In 1995 the club sold him to Milan.

Henry arrived from Monaco in January 1999. Juventus played him on the wing, and he scored 3 goals in 16 Serie A games.

That August, Arsenal bought him for about £11 million. Arsène Wenger, who had coached him at Monaco, moved him into the centre.

He went on to score 228 goals for Arsenal, a club record.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Before Henry ever joined Juventus, the two had already shared a pitch, in a World Cup quarter-final. France played Italy at the Stade de France on 3 July 1998.

It ended 0–0 and went to penalties. Baggio took Italy's first. Henry took one for France. Both scored.

France won the shoot-out 4–3. Four years after his miss in the 1994 final, Baggio had stepped up and scored.

Here's what stays with me.

Arsenal's statue of Henry shows him on his knees, as he was that day against Spurs.

Juventus had both of these men and sold both.

Signing talent is the easy part. Knowing what you have is harder.
