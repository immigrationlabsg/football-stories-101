On 16 November 1988, Roberto Baggio played for Italy for the first time. He was 21. Italy beat the Netherlands 1–0 in a friendly.

On 16 November 2003, Lionel Messi played for Barcelona's first team for the first time, in a friendly at Porto. He was 16.

Same date. Two debuts. 15 years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Messi's first game was also the first game at Porto's new stadium, the Estádio do Dragão.

Barcelona's side that night included several youth players. Messi came on in the second half, for Fernando Navarro. Porto won 2–0.

His first official game came 11 months later, a 1–0 win at Espanyol. By then he was 17.

Baggio's road had been slower.

In 1985, aged 18, he badly injured his right knee playing for Vicenza against Rimini. He was out for more than a year.

Rimini's coach that day was Arrigo Sacchi. Two years later, he took over at Milan.

When Baggio did reach the Italy team, his first opponents were the champions of Europe. The Netherlands had won Euro 88 that June, with Ruud Gullit and Marco van Basten scoring in the final.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Van Basten played against Baggio that night. So did Frank Rijkaard.

On the same date in 2003, Rijkaard was Barcelona's coach. He was the man who sent Messi on in Porto.

One man was on the pitch for the first debut and in charge for the second.

And Porto's coach that night was José Mourinho. Six months later, his team beat Monaco 3–0 to win the Champions League.

So both debuts came against champions of Europe: one reigning, one in waiting.

Baggio won the Ballon d'Or in 1993, at Juventus. Messi won his eighth in 2023.

Here's what stays with me.

One had come back from a bad knee injury. The other was 16, playing on the night a stadium opened.

Both started out against champions.

And the same man was there to see both first steps.
