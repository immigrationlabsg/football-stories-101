# The owner's taste in hooks and bodies

The owner asked for this on 19 Nov 2026: "take note of my taste for the hooks and body as well, as it is an important reference point in the reel skill." Read this before pitching hooks or writing any script, and check every draft against it before it reaches the owner. Add each new verdict here (approved or rejected, with the owner's words), and replace any point that a newer verdict beats.

Source of the hook and body ideas: Jack Craig, "I BLEW UP a Beginner's YouTube Channel in 7 Days" (https://youtu.be/sKejab-4Rmw), the video ideation section, about 6:30 to 8:30. The owner picked two ideas from it.

## Hooks

1. **The hook plants a question; it never answers what happens.** In the video's words, a hook that states the outcome "answers the question for them. There's no reason for them to actually watch the video." The viewer is put into an open loop that only the end of the Reel closes. This is not the same as giving away the ending as a headline.
2. **The answer must be something fans don't already know.** This is the owner's hardest test. If a United fan can answer the hook from last week's headlines, it fails, however neat the wording is. The owner on the Carrick options: "All of these smell like something any man utd fans would already know the answer to." Test every hook by asking: could a fan of this club answer it now? If yes, drop it.
3. **A plain question is fine when the question itself is surprising.** The owner chose a question hook for Carrick. That replaces the older advice that "a question alone is weaker than a stated fact" when the question sets up a contradiction fans can't resolve.
4. **Stay on the news.** On a trend Reel, the hook ties to why the person is in the news this week (the sacking, the new job, Sunday's game). A surprising personal fact from elsewhere in their life, however true, pulls the Reel off the news, and the owner rejected one.
5. **A clash of two records works.** Set up two facts that can't both survive the upcoming event, and let the closing question ask which one breaks.
6. **Give options and let the owner pick or mix.** The owner chooses among 4 to 6 hook options, asks for variations on a favourite, and may combine beats from two drafts. Always pitch several, each with what question it plants and why fans won't know the answer.

### Hooks the owner approved
- Carrick, 21 Nov 2026 (owner's own wording): "How did a manager sacked by a Championship club end up running Manchester United seven months later?" Most fans don't know Middlesbrough sacked him in June 2025. The comeback line answers it.
- Pochettino, 20 Nov 2026: "Mauricio Pochettino has never lost his first game at a new club. On Sunday, his first game is at the ground that beats him." Two records clash and Sunday decides.

### Hooks the owner rejected, and why
- "Less than six months after handing Michael Carrick a new contract, Manchester United sacked him." It was the headline everywhere, so it answers itself.
- "Michael Carrick has left Manchester United twice. Only once was it his choice." The same goes for every variation of it ("walked out once", "said goodbye twice", "guess which"): fans know both exits.
- "One night triggered almost two years of depression for Michael Carrick. And it wasn't last week's sacking." The owner called the whole script built on it "crap". It drifted off the news into his playing past, and the 2009 final is well known.
- "This week, Mauricio Pochettino takes over at Manchester United." This is a news statement that plants no question. It was replaced.

## Bodies

1. **The body follows the looped arc:** hook, then rising action, then conflict, then comeback, then rising action, then payoff that ties back to the hook, then the closing question. This replaces the earlier "trend Reel milestones" idea for the body. The conflict and the comeback are what keep people watching: a plain hook, rise, payoff is predictable. Label each line with its beat in the draft so the owner can see the shape, as on the Carrick and Pochettino drafts.
2. **The body stays on the thread of the news.** For a new manager's first big game, tell his record in that fixture, not his childhood. The owner changed Pochettino from his origin story (Bielsa at 1 a.m.) to "his history as an EPL manager against Liverpool", keeping the same closing question.
3. **A separate "loop closed" line is optional.** When the owner mixed the Pochettino drafts, they dropped it: the payoff line ("One win in eight Premier League visits. And his Manchester United debut is at Anfield.") closes the loop and leads into the question.
4. **Keep the career beats that lead to the news, in order.** The owner preferred the first Carrick body (2021 caretaker exit, Middlesbrough rise, Boro sacking, United's call, third place, the 2028 contract, one win in ten, sacked) over a version built on his personal life.
