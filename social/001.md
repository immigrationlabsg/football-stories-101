Ronaldo / The World Cup he won on two rebuilt knees

At 21 he won the Ballon d'Or. Then his right knee gave way twice in five months. Two years later he scored both goals in a World Cup final.

Ronaldo came off the bench in the second half of the first leg of the Coppa Italia final at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome on 12 April 2000. It was his first match since November. Within minutes, six by most accounts and seven by others, he shaped to turn and his right knee buckled beneath him. He went down clutching it. Lazio won the match 2–1, but the score hardly mattered: the patellar tendon in that knee, repaired after an injury against Lecce five months earlier, had ruptured completely.

Greatness in a striker is usually measured in what the player did. Ronaldo's case forces the other measurement, what the player was prevented from doing, and makes it impossible to settle. FIFA's vote named him the best player in the world at 20, and again after the knee had been rebuilt, which is a thing almost nobody manages once. The honest version of his story is not only the tragedy of the lost years in between, but what he did when they ended.

Which player's career do you still wonder about: what they did, or what their body stopped them doing?
