On 12 April 2000, Ronaldo came off the bench in Rome. It was his first match since November.

Within minutes, his right knee buckled. The tendon, repaired five months earlier, had ruptured completely.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Two years after that night, he scored both goals in a World Cup final.

The boy who did all that was nearly lost to football over a bus fare.

Ronaldo grew up supporting Flamengo, and Flamengo let him go. His youth coach says he missed training because he could not afford the bus. The club offered no help.

Cruzeiro took him instead. On 7 November 1993 he scored five in a 6–0 win. Then Europe: thirty league goals in his first season at PSV, then 47 goals in 49 games for Barcelona. In 1997 Inter paid a world-record fee.

And then his body started sending the bill.

In November 1999, against Lecce, his right knee gave way. Tendon ruptured. Surgery. Five months later came Rome, and he would not play another competitive match for 17 months.

The comeback was a run of false starts. The knee was rebuilt in Paris by the surgeon Gérard Saillant. His first league goal on it came on 9 December 2001, two years after Lecce. On the last day of that season Inter lost the title, and Ronaldo wept on the bench.

A few weeks later he was at the World Cup.

Then came the part that surprised me.

He shaved his head, all except a patch at the front. Years later he said it was deliberate. Once he turned up to training with it, nobody talked about his injury any more.

The haircut was a decoy.

In the final, Oliver Kahn, who had conceded one goal all tournament, failed to hold a shot from Rivaldo. Ronaldo scored from the rebound. Twelve minutes later he scored again. Brazil won 2–0.

Eight goals and the Golden Boot, on knees that had already failed him twice. They never stopped failing him. He retired in February 2011, aged 34. As he put it: "Mentally, I wanted to continue, but I have to acknowledge that I lost [the fight] to my body."

Here's what stays with me. We usually measure a striker by what he did. Ronaldo makes us count what he was prevented from doing, and the sum never settles.

He was the best in the world before the knee went, and again after it was rebuilt. Almost nobody manages that once.

Which player's career do you still wonder about: what they did, or what their body stopped them doing?
