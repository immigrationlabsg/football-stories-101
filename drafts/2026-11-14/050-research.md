## Claims (two independent sources each; Wikipedia used only as a pointer)

1. **July 2000: Pérez stood for president and was not expected to win.** (body)
   - ESPN, Riquelme/Haaland election piece, https://www.espn.com/story/_/id/48960585 (opened): "Perez was, back then, distinctly the second favorite in the race to win those Madrid presidential elections"; "It was on July 11, 2000, that Perez held a news conference".
   - Bleacher Report, https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2496426-luis-figo-to-real-madrid-the-transfer-that-launched-the-galacticos-era (opened): Sanz "was a shoo-in for re-election"; Pérez "was announced as president on July 16, 2000."

2. **His promise: if elected, he would sign Luís Figo, Barcelona's best player.** (body, fact 1, hook, dek)
   - ESPN (above): "I want you to know that if I'm made President of Real Madrid, Figo WILL become a Madrid player!"; "Luis Figo was by far Barcelona's best player".
   - Sports Illustrated, https://www.si.com/soccer/real-madrid-long-history-blockbuster-election-signings (opened): "Back in 2000, Pérez won his first term after promising to deliver Fígo—then contracted to arch-rivals Barcelona"; "The jaw-dropping promise of taking the league's best player from Madrid's greatest rivals".

3. **If he failed, he would pay the members' fees for a season himself.** (body, fact 1, hook)
   - Bleacher Report (above, citing Alfredo Relaño's book): he "would pay their membership fees the following year if he failed to sign Figo".
   - Tribal Football "Celebrating the Galácticos", read in syndication at https://newswav.com/article/celebrating-the-galacticos-a-real-madrid-team-that-marked-an-era-A2303_UjxeSc (opened; the tribalfootball.com original returned 404): "he would cover that season's membership fees for all of the club members himself."
   - Third, slightly different wording: ESPN (above): "the guarantee that he'd pay the Madrid socios their season ticket if his words weren't fulfilled".
   - Sources differ on fees vs season ticket (and Sportskeeda describes free entry), so the text says only "pay the members' fees for a season himself", which the first two support word for word. Not in the text: who would pay, how.

4. **He won; eight days later Figo was unveiled in Madrid.** (body)
   - Bleacher Report (above): "announced as president on July 16, 2000"; "Luis Figo was unveiled at Real Madrid on July 24, 2000."
   - Newswav/Tribal Football (above): Florentino "won, and a week later Figo was presented as Real Madrid's newest player."
   - RTÉ, 24 July 2000, https://www.rte.ie/sport/soccer/2000/0724/150480-soccer4 (opened): Figo "tonight became the world's most expensive footballer when he joined Real Madrid".

5. **Barcelona could not stop it: a buy-out clause of 10 billion pesetas, paid by Madrid; a world-record fee.** (body, fact 2)
   - ESPN (above): "the 'buyout' clause that had been inserted in his contract ... 10 billion pesetas in those days, equivalent to €60 million"; Barcelona "had no actual power to block the move".
   - Bleacher Report (above): Pérez deposited 10 billion pesetas, "which was the value of Figo's buyout clause and a world-record transfer fee."
   - RTÉ (above): "The fee was determined by the buy-out clause inserted in the Portuguese star's contract"; "the world's most expensive footballer".
   - Dropped: sterling/euro conversions (sources give £37m, £44m, €60m, €62m). The pesetas figure is consistent, so only it is used.

6. **That year Figo also won the Ballon d'Or.** (body)
   - RSSSF, https://rsssf.org/miscellaneous/europa-poy00.html (opened): 2000 winner "Luis Figo (Portugal), Real Madrid, 197 points"; runner-up Zidane, 181.
   - Topend Sports, https://www.topendsports.com/sport/soccer/awards/ballondor-2000.htm (opened): Luís Figo won the 2000 Ballon d'Or, club "Barcelona / Real Madrid".
   - Kept to "that year" (the exact presentation date, 19 Dec 2000 on Wikipedia, has no second source opened).

7. **The man he beat, Lorenzo Sanz, had just won two Champions Leagues in three seasons; the Figo promise helped beat him.** (body)
   - ESPN (above): "the incumbent president Lorenzo Sanz, had been racking up Champions League wins (1998 and 2000)"; the Figo promise and the guarantee "utterly galvanized the voting."
   - SI (above): "ahead of the incumbent Lorenzo Sanz, who had delivered two Champions League titles during his term."
   - Dropped: winning margin (ESPN "over 3,000 votes"; Bleacher Report "a few hundred votes": they disagree).

8. **Zidane from Juventus in 2001, Ronaldo from Inter in 2002, Beckham from Manchester United in 2003.** (body, fact 3)
   - Newswav/Tribal Football (above): "The signing of the Portuguese was followed a year later by that of Zinedine Zidane from Juventus", Ronaldo and Beckham "in 2002 and 2003".
   - Soccerway/Flashscore, https://www.soccerway.com/news/kylian-mbappe-becomes-the-latest-in-a-long-line-of-real-madrid-galacticos/Ms286WlA (opened): "Zinedine Zidane (2001 ...)", "Ronaldo Nazario (2002 ...)", "David Beckham (2003 ...)".
   - CBS Sports, https://www.cbssports.com/soccer/news/ranking-real-madrids-top-10-galactico-signings-david-beckham-cristiano-ronaldo-jude-bellingham (opened): "Juve's Zinedine Zidane ... (2001)", "United's David Beckham ... (2003)".
   - Ronaldo from Inter: VOA, 31 Aug 2002 (search excerpt, https://www.voanews.com/a/a-13-a-2002-08-31-25-soccer-66293497/541397.html): Real Madrid agreed to sign him from Inter "just hours before the European transfer deadline"; Daily Star 2 Sep 2002 (search excerpt): the club confirmed the transfer on its website.
   - Dropped: all fees (sources disagree).

9. **The plan's name, Zidanes y Pavones: world stars alongside academy graduates such as Francisco Pavón, a young centre-back.** (body, fact 3)
   - These Football Times, https://thesefootballtimes.co/2015/01/26/remembering-florentino-perezs-pavones-the-players-real-madrid-forgot/ (opened): "Perez's concept was originally called 'Zidanes y Pavones'", pairing "world class signings like Zidane" with "home-grown talent"; "Francisco Pavón was once considered a promising young central defender."
   - Colgados por el Fútbol, https://colgadosporelfutbol.com/en/zidanes-y-pavones/ (opened): "The term 'Zidanes and Pavones' arises from the fusion of two representative names"; "Francisco Pavón, a central defender trained in the lower categories of Real Madrid"; "Zidanes" world stars, "Pavones" young homegrown players.

10. **At first it worked: league 2001, Champions League 2002, league 2003.** (body)
    - UEFA, 27 Feb 2006, https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/01b2-0ea845bcf804-dee0f09fc517-1000--perez-steps-down-from-madrid-post/ (opened): "Madrid won the UEFA Champions League in 2002 as well as Spanish titles in 2001 and 2003".
    - The Irish Times, https://www.irishtimes.com/sport/soccer/perez-throws-in-the-towel-1.1187138 (opened): under Pérez Real "won the Primera Liga title twice and the Champions League once".

11. **Within two days of the 2003 title, the club dismissed its coach, Vicente del Bosque.** (body, fact 4)
    - Sky Sports, https://www.skysports.com/football/news/2268910/real-axe-del-bosque-and-hierro (opened): "REAL MADRID have sacked coach Vicente Del Bosque, just one day after the tactician led the capital club to La Liga title."
    - SI, https://www.si.com/soccer/2019/07/05/vicente-del-bosque-unluckiest-manager-world-who-led-spain-immortality (opened): "On 24 June 2003, less than 48 hours after they had won La Liga title on the final day of the season".
    - Sources differ (one day / under 48 hours; some call it non-renewal), so the text says "within two days" and "dismissed". Dropped: Hierro also leaving (Sky only).

12. **That summer, as Beckham arrived, Claude Makélélé left for Chelsea.** (body, fact 4)
    - The National, https://www.thenationalnews.com/sport/blues-can-drive-on-to-glory-1.523899 (opened): Zidane spoke "in 2003 after learning that Real Madrid's newest Galactico David Beckham" would never appear alongside Makélélé, "who had joined Chelsea".
    - These Football Times (above): Makélélé left for Chelsea; "The decision may have made the club richer, but it left the team imbalanced."
    - Chelsea FC (search excerpt; page needs login when opened): Makélélé was "Chelsea's 11th and final signing in the dramatic summer transfer window of 2003".
    - "the midfielder who won the ball back for the stars" is description, consistent with The National ("engine") and World Soccer Talk ("the covering of Makelele, sitting deep and giving freedom to the rest of the side").
    - Dropped: Makélélé's pay dispute and Pérez's reported reply about him (living persons; not needed).

13. **He won the league there in 2005 and 2006.** (body)
    - The National (above): Makélélé helped Chelsea win "back-to-back league titles, an FA Cup and a League Cup in his first three seasons in London".
    - World Soccer Talk, https://worldsoccertalk.com/news/the-greatest-teams-chelsea-fc-2004-2006-20150225-CMS-131260.html (opened): "In Mourinho's first season at the club, Chelsea won the Premier League title with a record total of 95 points"; "In the 2005-2006 ... defended the Premier League title", with Makélélé in midfield.
    - Premier League player profile (search excerpt, premierleague.com/players/2454): champion 2004/05 and 2005/06.

14. **Quote (the only one): Zidane: "Why put another layer of gold paint on the Bentley when you are losing the entire engine?"** (body)
    - The National (above), verbatim: "Why put another layer of gold paint on the Bentley when you are losing the entire engine," said Zinedine Zidane in 2003.
    - These Football Times (above), verbatim: Zidane said, "Why put another layer of gold paint on the Bentley when you are losing the entire engine?"
    - Note: other outlets paraphrase it ("another coat of paint", "taking out the engine"); the text uses the wording the two sources share exactly.

15. **By early 2006, Madrid had won no major trophy since that 2003 title.** (body, fact 5, dek)
    - UEFA (above): Madrid "have not lifted a major trophy since."
    - The Irish Times (above): "the club have not claimed any major silverware since winning the league in 2003."
    - Note: Madrid won the Spanish Super Cup in August 2003, so the text always says "major trophy", never "no trophy" or "trophyless".

16. **February 2006: Arsenal won 1–0 at the Bernabéu through a Thierry Henry goal.** (body)
    - UEFA (above): "Madrid's defeat at Mallorca followed their 1-0 home reverse against Arsenal FC" in the Champions League first knockout round first leg.
    - CBS Sports, https://www.cbssports.com/soccer/news/real-madrid-vs-arsenal-a-look-back-at-thierry-henrys-iconic-goal-in-2005-06-champions-league (opened): "Arsenal notched a 1-0 win at Real Madrid"; Henry "finished things off" in the 47th minute, "at the Santiago Bernabeu".
    - Date (21 Feb 2006) seen in search excerpts only; the text says "In February", which both sources support.

    - The Irish Times (above): "Sunday's 2-1 defeat at struggling Real Mallorca saw Madrid fall 10 points behind leaders" (Barcelona).
    - Mail & Guardian, 28 Feb 2006, https://mg.co.za/article/2006-02-28-real-madrid-president-quits-to-boost-club (search excerpt): the 2–1 defeat at Mallorca, with the club 10 points behind Barcelona.

    - The Irish Times (above), dated 27 Feb 2006: Pérez "tonight resigned"; "I believe that the club needs a change of direction."
