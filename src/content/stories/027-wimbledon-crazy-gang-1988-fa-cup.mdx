In the second half of the 1988 FA Cup final, Wimbledon were 1–0 up against Liverpool, the new league champions.

Then Clive Goodyear challenged John Aldridge in the area. Penalty.

No goalkeeper had ever saved a penalty in an FA Cup final.

Aldridge stuttered and shot to the goalkeeper's left. Dave Beasant was already diving that way, and pushed it out.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The save looked like instinct. It was homework.

Beasant, Wimbledon's captain, had studied Aldridge's penalties on television all season. He noticed the stutter, and that goalkeepers committed early when Aldridge paused.

Hold your ground, and Aldridge goes left.

It was not even Wimbledon's first famous penalty save.

In January 1975, Southern League Wimbledon played Leeds United in the FA Cup. Late on, Dickie Guy saved a Peter Lorimer penalty.

It finished 0–0. Wimbledon lost the replay to a goal off the knee of one of their own defenders.

His name was Dave Bassett. Remember him.

In 1977 they joined the Football League in place of Workington.

They did not play their way in. They were elected.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Bassett became manager in 1981. In 1982–83 they won the Fourth Division with 98 points, a record under three points for a win.

They reached the First Division, and by early September 1986 they were top of it.

They finished sixth, and Bassett left. A year later, under Bobby Gould, came Wembley.

Before half-time, Lawrie Sanchez headed in a Dennis Wise free kick. Beasant's save kept it that way.

As John Motson put it on the BBC: "The Crazy Gang have beaten the Culture Club."

And the defender whose knee knocked them out in 1975 built the team that won it.

Here's what stays with me.

Attitude did not save that penalty. A goalkeeper who knew more than his opponent did. Clubs without money can still choose to do the homework.

For one afternoon, that beat the best team in England.
