On Sunday he started for Ravenna, in Italy's Serie C, against Forlì. He was in the move for Giulio Donati's goal. Ravenna won 2–0. He went off at half-time.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

This is the man who made Real Madrid's own fans give him a standing ovation.

It happened 21 years ago today, on 19 November 2005, at the Santiago Bernabéu.

Vanderlei Luxemburgo's Real Madrid fielded Zinedine Zidane, Ronaldo, Raúl, Roberto Carlos and David Beckham. Frank Rijkaard's Barcelona went ahead early through Samuel Eto'o.

Just before the hour, Ronaldinho picked up the ball on the left, ran at Sergio Ramos, went past him and Iván Helguera, and beat Iker Casillas.

With 13 minutes left, he beat Ramos again and placed it in the far corner.

Barcelona led 3–0. And many of the home fans rose to applaud.

But here's where it gets interesting.

The Barcelona player before him was Diego Maradona, in 1983.

In June 1983, in a League Cup final at the Bernabéu, Maradona rounded the goalkeeper. A defender, Juan José, slid in to stop him and crashed into the post. Maradona scored, and the home fans applauded. Barcelona went on to win the cup.

It took 22 years to happen again.

Only a handful of Barcelona players have ever had that moment there.

On Sunday, 11 years after his last club game, with Fluminense, he walked out again.

Here's what stays with me.

The Bernabéu stood for him at 25. At 46, he's still worth the ticket.

Applause from a rival crowd cannot be bought or asked for. It has to be earned, in their stadium, against their team.
