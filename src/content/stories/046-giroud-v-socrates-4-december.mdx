On 4 December 2022, Olivier Giroud scored against Poland in the last 16 of the World Cup in Qatar. Kylian Mbappé slid the pass through a minute before half-time.

It was Giroud's 52nd goal for France, one more than Thierry Henry. France won 3–1.

On 4 December 2011, Sócrates, Brazil's captain at the 1982 World Cup, died in São Paulo. He was 57.

Two Sundays. Eleven years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Four years before his record, Giroud won the World Cup in Russia without scoring.

He did not have a single shot on target all tournament. France still beat Croatia 4–2 in the final.

Sócrates had the opposite luck.

He was a qualified doctor. At the 1982 World Cup he scored against Italy in Barcelona, and Brazil still lost 3–2 to a Paolo Rossi hat-trick.

In 1986 he scored a penalty against Poland in the last 16: the same round and the same opponent as Giroud's record night.

Next came France in the quarter-final. Sócrates took Brazil's first kick in the shootout. Joël Bats saved it, and France went through.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Time reported that Sócrates once told an interviewer he wanted to die on a Sunday when Corinthians won the league title.

4 December 2011 was a Sunday. Later that day, Corinthians, his old club, drew 0–0 with Palmeiras and won the Brazilian league for the fifth time.

During the minute's silence before kick-off, their players raised clenched right fists: his trademark salute.

Giroud held the France record until 2026. That June, Mbappé, the man who set up his 52nd, passed Giroud's final total of 57.

Here's what stays with me.

One man won a World Cup without a shot on target. The other scored at two and never won one.

Their 4 Decembers both fell on a Sunday.

Medals and goals do not always arrive together. Sometimes the ending is what people remember.
