On 31 August 2008 Barcelona opened their league season at Numancia, just promoted. They lost 1–0.

Next they drew 1–1 at home to Racing Santander.

The man in charge had never coached in the top flight.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Pep Guardiola’s entire coaching record was one season with Barça B, in the fourth tier of Spanish football.

At his first press conference he said he did not want Ronaldinho, Deco or Samuel Eto’o.

Instead he brought up two players from that B team: Sergio Busquets and Pedro Rodríguez.

Then he lost his first game.

After the Racing match, Andrés Iniesta walked into his office unprompted. As Iniesta later told FourFourTwo, he wanted to reassure his coach: “Don’t worry, everything will be fine.”

He was right. Barcelona won 19 of their next 20 league games.

Then came the part that surprised me.

In the Champions League semi-final at Chelsea, Barcelona were 1–0 down and down to ten men. In stoppage time Iniesta scored from the edge of the area.

In the final in Rome they beat Manchester United 2–0. With the league and the Copa del Rey already won, that was the treble.

Barcelona added both Super Cups, then beat Estudiantes in the Club World Cup final. Lionel Messi bundled in the winner with his chest.

All six trophies they entered, in a calendar year. No club had done that before.

Xavi Hernández, Iniesta and Messi had all come through La Masia, the club’s academy.

In 2011 they beat Manchester United in the final again, 3–1 at Wembley. Pedro scored the first. Busquets set up the third.

The two reserves from 2008 had made a goal each.

Guardiola left in 2012, saying four years had been an eternity. His last match brought his 14th trophy, out of a possible 19.

Here's what stays with me.

One of football’s great eras began with a defeat at a newly promoted side. Its coach trusted the players he already knew. And it turned on a midfielder knocking on an office door.
