On 31 August 2008 Barcelona opened their league season away at Numancia, a club that had just been promoted, and lost 1–0 to a penalty in the 13th minute. In their next league game they drew 1–1 with Racing Santander at the Camp Nou. The man in charge had never coached in the top flight. A year earlier Pep Guardiola had been coaching Barcelona’s reserve team in the fourth tier of Spanish football.

After the Racing match, one of his midfielders decided to pay him a visit.

> We lost the first league game of the season, then drew with Racing Santander. Spontaneously, I decided to go to Pep’s office to reassure him. ‘Don’t worry,’ I told him, ‘everything will be fine.’
> — Andrés Iniesta, interview with FourFourTwo, 2020

Iniesta was right. Barcelona won 19 of their next 20 league games.

## The B-team coach

Guardiola was a former Barcelona player. At 21 he had been in midfield for Johan Cruyff’s side when it beat Sampdoria 1–0 at Wembley in 1992, after extra time, to win the club’s first European Cup. His coaching record, though, was one season long. In 2007–08 he took Barça B, then in the Tercera División, to the top of their Catalan group and through the play-offs into Segunda División B.

The president, Joan Laporta, announced Guardiola as the successor to Frank Rijkaard on 8 May 2008.

At his first press conference, on 17 June 2008, Guardiola said that he did not want Ronaldinho, Deco or Samuel Eto’o in his squad. Ronaldinho joined Milan that July. Eto’o stayed one more season and scored in a Champions League final. In their place Guardiola brought up two players he had coached in the B team: a defensive midfielder called Sergio Busquets and a winger called Pedro Rodríguez. Busquets made his first-team debut on 13 September 2008. Both had been part of the B team that won promotion a few months earlier.

It was, in other words, a coach with one season of experience, three divisions below La Liga, dismissing the club’s most famous player and replacing him with reserves. Had Barcelona kept losing in September, the decision would have looked reckless.

## 2009

On 2 May 2009 Barcelona went to the Santiago Bernabéu. Real Madrid scored first through Gonzalo Higuaín after 14 minutes and Sergio Ramos added a second early in the second half. In between and after, Barcelona scored six: two for Thierry Henry, the first only four minutes after Higuaín’s, one for Carles Puyol, two for Lionel Messi and one for Gerard Piqué. Final score 2–6. That night, for the first time by UEFA’s account, Guardiola had Messi start on the right and drop deep into the middle. The position would become known as the false nine.

Days later the season nearly collapsed. After a goalless first leg at the Camp Nou, Barcelona were 1–0 down to Chelsea at Stamford Bridge from Michael Essien’s ninth-minute goal, and down to ten men after Éric Abidal was sent off midway through the second half. In stoppage time Iniesta shot from the edge of the area, and the ball went past Petr Čech. Barcelona went through on away goals.

The final, in Rome on 27 May 2009, was against Manchester United, the holders, and it was billed as Messi against Cristiano Ronaldo. After ten minutes Iniesta played in Eto’o, who cut inside Nemanja Vidić and squeezed a shot past Edwin van der Sar. In the 70th minute Xavi Hernández crossed and Messi scored with a looping header. Barcelona won 2–0. A fortnight earlier they had beaten Athletic Club 4–1 in the Copa del Rey final, coming from a goal down with Messi, Bojan Krkić and Xavi scoring in the second half, and the league title was also theirs. Guardiola had won the treble in his first season as a top-flight coach.

He was not finished with the year. Barcelona added the Spanish Super Cup and the UEFA Super Cup, and on 19 December 2009, in Abu Dhabi, they played Estudiantes of Argentina in the final of the Club World Cup. Estudiantes led almost to the end of normal time, until Pedro equalised from Piqué’s header across goal. In extra time, with about ten minutes left, Messi bundled the winner in with his chest. It was the first Club World Cup in the club’s history. Barcelona had won all six trophies they competed for in a calendar year, which no club had done before.

## The turn

The decision that made this team was the board’s choice to give the first team to a coach whose only experience was with the reserves, and Guardiola’s own choice to trust the players he knew from there. He had watched Busquets and Pedro every week of a season in the Tercera División and knew exactly what they could do; Xavi, Iniesta and Messi had all come through La Masia, the club’s academy, and Guardiola built the team around their passing rather than around a star signing.

The 2010 Ballon d’Or showed what that meant. Messi won it. Iniesta was second and Xavi third, all three of them products of La Masia.

The 2010–11 season produced the team’s two most famous nights. On 29 November 2010 Barcelona beat Real Madrid 5–0 at the Camp Nou, with goals from Xavi, Pedro, David Villa twice and the substitute Jeffrén. On 28 May 2011 they met Manchester United in the Champions League final again, this time at the rebuilt Wembley, on the site where Guardiola had won the European Cup as a player 19 years earlier. Pedro scored first, Wayne Rooney equalised, Messi put Barcelona back in front after half-time and Villa scored the third after a pass from Busquets. Barcelona won 3–1. The two reserves Guardiola had promoted in the summer of 2008 had made a goal each in a Champions League final. Sir Alex Ferguson said afterwards that, in his time as a manager, they were the best team he had faced, and he admitted that United had never really controlled Messi.

> No one has given us a hiding like that.
> — Sir Alex Ferguson, after the Champions League final, Wembley, 28 May 2011

## Four years

It ended quickly, and on Guardiola’s terms. In April 2012 Barcelona lost the Champions League semi-final to Chelsea, and on 27 April Guardiola announced that he would leave at the end of the season. He said that four years as Barcelona coach was an eternity and that he needed to recharge. His assistant, Tito Vilanova, was named as his successor the same day. By then the team had added a second Club World Cup, two years after the first, beating Santos 4–0 in Yokohama on 18 December 2011, with two goals from Messi and one each from Xavi and Cesc Fàbregas.

His last match was the Copa del Rey final against Athletic Club at the Vicente Calderón in Madrid on 25 May 2012, three years after the cup final that had started the treble. It was over inside 25 minutes: Pedro scored after three, Messi after 20 and Pedro again after 25. Barcelona won 3–0. It was his 14th trophy in four seasons, out of a possible 19: three league titles, two Champions Leagues, two Copas del Rey, three Spanish Super Cups, two UEFA Super Cups and two Club World Cups.

## What it tells us

Fourteen trophies in four years began with a defeat at a newly promoted club and a coach whose only experience was in the fourth tier. What made the difference was not a signing but a judgement: that the players already at the club, many of them from its own academy, were good enough if someone built the team around what they did best. It is a reminder that the hardest part of a great era is often the first month, and that it can depend on a few people choosing not to panic.
