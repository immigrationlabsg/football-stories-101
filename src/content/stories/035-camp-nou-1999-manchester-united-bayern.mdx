When the 90 minutes ran out at Camp Nou on 26 May 1999, Bayern Munich were beating Manchester United 1–0.

They had led since the sixth minute. They had hit the woodwork twice.

Three minutes later, United were champions of Europe.

Here’s what I didn’t know until recently.

Both United goals came from substitutes.

United had come to Barcelona chasing something no English club had ever won: the Treble. The Premier League and the FA Cup were already theirs.

They came without their captain. Roy Keane and Paul Scholes were suspended. Peter Schmeichel, in his last game for the club, wore the armband.

Mario Basler’s free kick put Bayern ahead.

In the 67th minute Alex Ferguson sent on Teddy Sheringham. With about ten minutes left, Ole Gunnar Solskjær came on too.

Neither change seemed to matter.

In the first minute of added time, Ryan Giggs mis-hit a shot back towards goal. Sheringham swept it in. He has since admitted he scuffed it.

1–1. Most people assumed extra time was coming.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The final was played on what would have been Sir Matt Busby’s 90th birthday.

In 1958 Busby’s young team were flying home from Belgrade when their plane crashed at Munich. Twenty-three people died, eight of them United players. Busby was given the last rites.

He rebuilt. In 1968 his team beat Benfica at Wembley to win the European Cup.

For the next 31 years, no United side won it again.

Back in Barcelona, extra time never came.

Barely two minutes later David Beckham took a corner. Sheringham flicked it on. Solskjær prodded it into the roof of the net.

Sheringham had been on for 24 minutes. Solskjær, on for about ten, needed one touch.

As Ferguson put it to the camera that night: “Football, bloody hell.”

Here’s what stays with me.

A final is not over when it looks over. It is over when the referee ends it.

Bayern were better for 90 minutes. United were better for three, and three was enough.
