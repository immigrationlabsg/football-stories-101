On the evening of Monday 2 May 2016, cars carrying Christian Fuchs, Robert Huth, Wes Morgan and Shinji Okazaki pulled up outside Jamie Vardy's house in Melton Mowbray, in Leicestershire. The Leicester City squad had gathered to watch a match they were not playing in. At Stamford Bridge, Tottenham Hotspur, the only team who could still catch them, had to beat Chelsea to keep the title race alive.

At half-time Tottenham led 2–0 through Harry Kane and Son Heung-min. Then Gary Cahill pulled one back, and late in the second half Eden Hazard, on as a substitute, scored the equaliser. The match finished 2–2. Footage of Leicester's players leaping on each other in front of Vardy's television was round the world within hours.

Their manager was not there. Claudio Ranieri had flown to Rome that day to have lunch with his mother, who was 96. Accounts differ on whether he saw the match live or landed after it was over. Leicester City were champions of England for the first time in their 132-year history.

Before the season started, bookmakers had offered 5,000 to 1 against it.

## Bottom at Christmas

Leicester's title was not built from strength. Twelve months earlier they had been bottom of the Premier League on Christmas Day, in their first season back in the top flight. By early April 2015 they had won only four of their first 29 league matches and were seven points from safety. Then, under Nigel Pearson, they won seven of their last nine, starting with a 2–1 win over West Ham on 4 April, and finished 14th. The club still calls it the greatest of great escapes.

Pearson did not get to build on it. On 30 June 2015 the club's Thai owners dismissed him, citing "fundamental differences in perspective". On 13 July they appointed Ranieri, an Italian who had once managed Chelsea, but whose last job had ended badly: he had been sacked as coach of Greece after a 1–0 home defeat by the Faroe Islands. Much of the reaction in England was sceptical.

Ranieri and the chairman agreed a target for the season: 40 points, the total generally reckoned enough to stay up.

## Bargains and a pizza

The team Ranieri inherited had been assembled cheaply and patiently. Its striker, Vardy, had begun his career in non-League football at Stocksbridge Park Steels, moved to FC Halifax Town in 2010 and to Fleetwood Town in 2011, and joined Leicester in May 2012 for a fee reported at £1 million, then a record for a non-League player. Its playmaker, Riyad Mahrez, had arrived from Le Havre in January 2014 for less than £500,000; reported figures vary. He had been spotted in France by the club's scout, Steve Walsh.

In August 2015 Walsh's department added a midfielder from Caen, N'Golo Kanté, for a fee most often reported at about £5.6 million, though figures vary. Ranieri later wrote that Kanté seemed to have batteries hidden in his shorts. By the end of the season he led the Premier League in both tackles and interceptions.

Vardy scored in the first weeks of the season, and kept scoring. On 28 November 2015, in a 1–1 draw at home to Manchester United, he scored after 24 minutes. It was his 11th consecutive Premier League match with a goal, one more than the record Ruud van Nistelrooy had set in 2003. The run had begun with a penalty at Bournemouth on 29 August.

The defence took longer. Two months into the season Leicester had still not kept a clean sheet, so Ranieri made the players an offer: keep one, and he would buy them pizza. On 24 October they beat Crystal Palace 1–0, Vardy scoring from Mahrez's pass. Ranieri took them to a pizzeria in the city centre and, instead of simply feeding them, made them roll the dough and choose the toppings themselves.

On 19 December a 3–2 win at Everton, with two penalties from Mahrez, guaranteed that Leicester would be top on Christmas Day. No Premier League side had gone from bottom at one Christmas to top at the next.

## The turn

The decisive choice of the season was a choice not to do something. At Chelsea, years earlier, Ranieri's habit of rotating his team had earned him the nickname the Tinkerman. At Leicester he arrived, watched recordings of every one of the club's matches from the previous season, and concluded that the side that had escaped relegation did not need reinventing.

> I told the players I trusted them and I would talk very little about tactics.
> — Claudio Ranieri, interview with Corriere della Sera, February 2016, as reported in English translation

He sensed, he said, that his players were wary of the Italian reputation for tactical lectures. He promised them at least two days off a week, and asked in return that they run as hard as he had seen them run at the end of the previous season. The system was simple: a compact 4–4–2 that sat deep, let opponents have the ball and broke at speed through Mahrez and Vardy. According to the Premier League's own analysis, Ranieri made only 33 changes to his starting line-up across the 38 league matches, the second-fewest by any Premier League champion and 20 fewer than any other title-winner this century. The same names appeared week after week: Kasper Schmeichel; Danny Simpson, Huth, Morgan, Fuchs; Mahrez, Danny Drinkwater, Kanté, Marc Albrighton; Okazaki and Vardy.

Opponents kept waiting for the run to end. On 6 February 2016 Leicester won 3–1 at Manchester City, Huth scoring twice and Mahrez once, and went clear at the top. Eight days later, at Arsenal, Vardy's penalty put them ahead, Simpson was sent off early in the second half, Theo Walcott equalised and Danny Welbeck headed the winner in stoppage time. It was their third league defeat of the season. It was also their last.

In April, with the title in sight and the whole country asking whether it could last, Ranieri wrote an essay explaining what he told supporters who said they were dreaming.

> We do not dream. We simply work hard.
> — Claudio Ranieri, "We Do Not Dream", The Players' Tribune, April 2016

## Champions

After the night at Vardy's house there were two matches left. On 7 May, at home to Everton, Andrea Bocelli sang before kick-off, Vardy scored twice and Andy King once in a 3–1 win, and Morgan, the captain, lifted the trophy with Ranieri beside him. Leicester finished with 81 points, ten more than Arsenal in second. Vardy scored 24 league goals and Mahrez 17. Mahrez was voted the PFA Players' Player of the Year; Vardy was the Football Writers' Footballer of the Year.

On the club's website, the night the title was won, Ranieri was frank about it.

> I never expected this when I arrived.
> — Claudio Ranieri, Leicester City website, 2 May 2016, as reported by AFP

Neither had the bookmakers. William Hill had taken 25 bets at 5,000 to 1 and reported a loss of about £2.2 million; Ladbrokes had taken 47 bets on Leicester before September, when the price had fallen to 1,500 to 1, and called it its biggest ante-post payout in 130 years. Industry estimates of the total loss ran above £25 million. In December 2015 Gary Lineker, the presenter of Match of the Day, had promised on Twitter that if Leicester won the league he would present the first Match of the Day of the next season in his underwear. In August 2016 he did, in a pair of white shorts.

## What it tells us

Odds measure what is usually true, and in a league where money buys points, 5,000 to 1 was not an unreasonable price. Leicester show what lies outside the model: a squad assembled from overlooked players, and a manager willing to leave a working team alone. Stability is not glamorous, but it is the one advantage a smaller club can afford.
