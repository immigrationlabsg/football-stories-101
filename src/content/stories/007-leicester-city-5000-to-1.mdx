On 2 May 2016, the Leicester City squad gathered at Jamie Vardy's house to watch a match they were not playing in.

Tottenham Hotspur had to beat Chelsea. At half-time they led 2–0. It finished 2–2.

Leicester were champions for the first time in their 132-year history.

Before the season, bookmakers made them 5,000 to 1.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

It was not a silly price. Twelve months earlier, Leicester had been bottom of the Premier League on Christmas Day.

They survived under Nigel Pearson. That summer the club dismissed him anyway.

In his place came Claudio Ranieri, whose last job had ended with the sack as coach of Greece after a home defeat by the Faroe Islands.

The squad was built on bargains. Vardy had begun in non-League football and joined from Fleetwood Town.

Riyad Mahrez had come from Le Havre for less than £500,000. N'Golo Kanté arrived from Caen.

Vardy scored in 11 consecutive Premier League matches, a record.

The defence took longer. So Ranieri offered the players pizza if they kept a clean sheet.

After a 1–0 win over Crystal Palace, he made them roll the dough themselves.

By Christmas they were top. No Premier League side had gone from bottom at one Christmas to top at the next.

Then came the part that surprised me.

At Chelsea, years earlier, Ranieri's habit of rotating his team had earned him the nickname the Tinkerman.

At Leicester he made only 33 changes to his starting line-up all season, the second-fewest by any Premier League champion.

On 7 May Wes Morgan, the captain, lifted the trophy. Leicester finished ten points clear of Arsenal.

William Hill had taken 25 bets at 5,000 to 1, and reported a loss of about £2.2 million.

Here's what stays with me.

The odds were not wrong. In a league where money buys points, 5,000 to 1 is a fair price.

What the odds cannot see is a manager willing to leave a working team alone.

Sometimes the bravest decision is to change nothing.
