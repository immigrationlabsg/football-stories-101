On the stroke of half-time in Munich on 30 May 1979, John Robertson curled a cross to the far post.

Trevor Francis met it with a diving header.

Nottingham Forest 1, Malmö 0. It stayed that way.

A club that had been in the Second Division two years earlier were champions of Europe.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The missing piece was not a player.

Brian Clough took over on 6 January 1975 with Forest, by Daniel Taylor's account, 13th in the Second Division. His first 18 months were dull.

In July 1976 Peter Taylor, his former assistant at Derby, came from Brighton to rejoin him.

Taylor was the scout and the judge of character. Clough was the voice.

In 1976–77 they went up in third, a single point clear. It was settled after their season ended, when Bolton lost to Wolves.

Promoted clubs are meant to survive.

Forest won the league, seven points ahead of Liverpool, the European champions.

Then came the part that surprised me.

They also won the League Cup that spring, with an 18-year-old reserve called Chris Woods in goal. Peter Shilton was cup-tied.

They went 42 league games unbeaten, a record nobody beat until Arsenal in 2004.

Robertson was already there when Clough arrived.

Clough once dismissed him as scruffy, unfit and uninterested. He became the player every move went through. As Clough later put it, Robertson was "a Picasso of our game".

Francis, signed for a fee Clough set at £999,999 so it would not go to his head, missed the earlier rounds. The final was his first European Cup match.

A year later they did it again.

Against Hamburg in Madrid, Forest sat deep, trusted Shilton and waited for Robertson. He cut inside and scored.

1–0 again.

Here's what stays with me.

Forest did not win because of money. They won because two people judged players by what they could become.

For a few years, the gap between the Second Division and the best team in Europe was mostly a question of who sat in the dugout.
