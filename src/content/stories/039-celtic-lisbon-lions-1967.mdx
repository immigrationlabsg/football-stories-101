In the tunnel in Lisbon, minutes before the European Cup final, Bertie Auld started to sing.

Across the tunnel on 25 May 1967 stood Inter, champions of Europe in 1964 and 1965. Celtic had never played in a European final.

Auld began the Celtic Song. His team-mates joined in. The Italians looked baffled.

Ninety minutes later, the singers were the first British team to win the European Cup.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Every man in Celtic's starting eleven was born within about 30 miles of Celtic Park.

Ronnie Simpson, the goalkeeper, was born in Glasgow. Billy McNeill, the captain, in Bellshill. The furthest away was Bobby Lennox, from Saltcoats on the Ayrshire coast.

Take Lennox out, and the champions of Europe were drawn from a bus ride.

The man who shaped them was a local too.

Jock Stein was born near Hamilton in 1922. He worked as a miner and played part-time.

In March 1965 he became Celtic's manager, the first Protestant in the club's history. Celtic had not won a major trophy since 1957.

But here's where it gets interesting.

By Lisbon they had already won the league, the Scottish Cup, the League Cup and the Glasgow Cup. Lisbon would make it five out of five.

Inter defended deep, and arrived without Luis Suárez and Jair da Costa. In the seventh minute Sandro Mazzola scored a penalty.

For an hour Celtic could not get through. Stein's answer was to send his full-backs forward.

In the 63rd minute Jim Craig, the right-back, rolled the ball across. Tommy Gemmell, the left-back, met it first time. 1–1.

In the 84th minute Bobby Murdoch's long shot was deflected in by Stevie Chalmers.

Celtic 2, Inter 1.

In the chaos afterwards, McNeill was separated from his team. He climbed up and lifted the trophy alone.

Here's what stays with me.

A team found within a few miles of its own ground beat the champions of Europe. Their manager was a former miner.

Nobody has done it like that since.
