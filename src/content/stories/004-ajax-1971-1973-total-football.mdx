Five minutes into the European Cup final at Wembley on 2 June 1971, Piet Keizer swung a cross in from the left and Dick van Dijk, a forward most of the crowd had never heard of, headed it past the Panathinaikos goalkeeper. Ajax Amsterdam led 1–0. On the Greek bench sat their coach, Ferenc Puskás, who had scored four goals in a European Cup final on a Scottish pitch 11 years earlier and now watched a team do to his side what Real Madrid had once done to everyone. A second goal arrived three minutes from the end, a shot from Arie Haan that deflected in off a defender. Ajax 2, Panathinaikos 0.

The captain who lifted the trophy was a 31-year-old Yugoslav centre-back called Velibor Vasović. The player everyone remembered was a 24-year-old from the east of the city who had not scored and did not need to. Johan Cruyff had spent 90 minutes appearing wherever the game was about to happen, and the rest of European football had started to wonder how he knew.

## A club near the bottom

The dynasty began with a rescue. In January 1965 Ajax were near the foot of the Eredivisie and appointed as coach a former centre-forward of theirs, a 36-year-old physical education teacher called Rinus Michels. Ajax finished that season 13th in a 16-team league. The following May they were champions, and they won the title again in 1967 and 1968, with a cup double in 1967.

Michels did two things that were unusual for the time. He made the players fitter than anybody they faced, with training that was openly modelled on military conditioning, and he insisted that fitness was not an end but a means: it bought the energy to press high up the pitch and to keep a defensive line so far forward that opponents were repeatedly caught offside. He had an unsentimental way of describing the business he was in.

> Football is war.
> — Rinus Michels

The first time the rest of Europe noticed was 7 December 1966, when Liverpool came to Amsterdam for a European Cup second-round tie and lost 5–1 in fog so thick that the Liverpool manager Bill Shankly walked on to the pitch during the match to talk to his players. In 1969 Ajax reached the final itself and were taught a lesson, losing 4–1 to Milan in Madrid with Pierino Prati scoring three. In 1970 the European Cup went to the Netherlands for the first time, but it was Feyenoord of Rotterdam, under Ernst Happel, who won it, and Feyenoord who took the league title in 1971. Ajax's answer was to win everything else.

## Three finals, no goals conceded

The side Michels took to Wembley in 1971 had an architecture that is now taught in coaching courses. Heinz Stuy was in goal. Wim Suurbier and Ruud Krol were full-backs who attacked as much as they defended. Vasović and Barry Hulshoff were the centre-backs, with Hulshoff encouraged to carry the ball into midfield. Johan Neeskens, 19 that summer, was the midfielder who ran further and tackled harder than anyone; Gerrie Mühren and Haan passed; Sjaak Swart and Keizer were the wingers; Cruyff was nominally the centre-forward and in practice whatever the moment required.

What made the system work was not the formation but the permission. When a full-back went forward, a midfielder dropped into his space; when Cruyff drifted to the wing, someone else became the striker. The pitch was treated as a problem of space rather than of positions, and the players talked about it constantly.

> We discussed space the whole time. Cruyff always talked about where people should run, where they should stand, when they should not be moving.
> — Barry Hulshoff, quoted in David Winner's Brilliant Orange

Michels left for Barcelona after the 1971 final. The man the Ajax board hired to replace him was a quietly spoken Romanian, Ștefan Kovács, who had won the title with Steaua Bucharest and whom nobody in Amsterdam had expected. Kovács made a decision that looks easy in hindsight and was not: he loosened the discipline and let the players run the system themselves. The result was the best club season anyone had seen.

In 1971–72 Ajax won the league, won the Dutch cup, and won the European Cup for the second time, beating Inter 2–0 in the final in Rotterdam on 31 May 1972 with both goals from Cruyff: a close-range finish after a rebound and a header from a Keizer cross. That autumn they beat Independiente of Argentina to win the Intercontinental Cup, 1–1 in Buenos Aires and 3–0 in Amsterdam, and in January 1973 they beat Rangers in the first European Super Cup. In the spring of 1973 they put four goals past Bayern Munich in a quarter-final first leg in Amsterdam, beat Real Madrid in the semi-final, and on 30 May 1973 in Belgrade beat Juventus 1–0 with a header from Johnny Rep after five minutes. Three consecutive European Cups, three finals, no goal conceded in any of them. Only Real Madrid in the 1950s had won three in a row.

## The vote

Kovács left in the summer of 1973 to take charge of the French national team. Before the new season the squad held its annual vote for captain. Cruyff, who had held the armband, expected to keep it. The players chose Keizer instead. Cruyff took it as a verdict, and within weeks he had followed Michels to Barcelona for a fee of about six million guilders, roughly £922,000, a world record. Neeskens joined him a year later. Ajax won the league again in 1977 but did not win the European Cup again until 1995.

Cruyff spent the rest of his life insisting that the way Ajax had played was a matter of thinking rather than of money or talent, and he had the results to make the argument stick: a World Cup final with the Netherlands in 1974, a league title with Barcelona as a player, and later, as a coach, the Barcelona team that won the club's first European Cup in 1992 and became the template for the one that won it four times in the next two decades.

> Playing football is very simple, but playing simple football is the hardest thing there is.
> — Johan Cruyff

## The turn

The decision that made the dynasty was Michels's, in 1965, to treat conditioning not as a virtue in itself but as the price of a style. Everything that followed, the pressing, the offside line, the interchange of positions, depended on 11 players who could run for 90 minutes at a tempo their opponents could not sustain. The decision that ended it was the players' own, eight years later, in a vote that was never about football. Cruyff and Keizer had grown up together at the club and never fully repaired the breach. Michels died in 2005, Kovács in 1995, Cruyff in March 2016 and Neeskens in 2024. Krol, Haan, Swart and Rep still turn up at the stadium that now bears Cruyff's name.

## What it tells us

Dynasties are built on ideas and dismantled by people. Ajax's three European Cups came from a way of thinking about space that one coach imposed and a second coach was wise enough to leave alone, and it fell apart not because anyone found a tactical answer but because a dressing room made a decision about status. The idea, though, outlived the team: it travelled with Michels and Cruyff to Barcelona, and it is still visible, half a century on, in the way the best sides in the world defend by attacking.
