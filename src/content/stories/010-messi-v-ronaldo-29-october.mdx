On 29 October 2011, Lionel Messi scored a hat-trick before half-time at Camp Nou. Barcelona beat Mallorca 5–0.

On 29 October 2016, Cristiano Ronaldo scored a hat-trick at Alavés. Real Madrid had been a goal down after seven minutes. They won 4–1.

Same date. Same answer. Five years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Both hat-tricks ended a goal drought.

Messi's was short. He had gone three games without scoring, and in Spain that was enough to cause concern.

Against Mallorca he scored a penalty, then two more before the break. Isaac Cuenca, with his first goal for the club, and Dani Alves added the rest.

It was his 13th career hat-trick, and it took him to 199 goals for Barcelona.

Three days later, in Plzeň, he scored another hat-trick. The first goal that night was his 200th for the club.

Ronaldo's was a slow start. He had 4 goals in 9 games that season.

The day before Alavés, Zinedine Zidane said his striker was not anxious, only perhaps angry that the goals had stopped.

At Alavés, Ronaldo equalised from the penalty spot and put Real ahead before half-time.

Then the goalkeeper, Fernando Pacheco, saved his second penalty.

Two minutes from time, Ronaldo scored again anyway. The win kept Real top of La Liga.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The seasons that followed went in opposite directions.

Messi finished 2011–12 with 73 goals in all competitions, beating Gerd Müller's European record of 67.

Barcelona still lost the league to Real Madrid. They went out of the Champions League to Chelsea in the semi-finals.

Ronaldo's slow start ended in Cardiff. He scored twice in the Champions League final.

Real Madrid won the league and the European Cup in the same season for the first time since 1958.

Here's what stays with me.

A few quiet games are enough to start the doubts.

Both men answer them the same way: three goals in one night.

Then one season ends with a record, and the other with the double.
