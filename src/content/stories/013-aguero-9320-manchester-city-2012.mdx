At the Stadium of Light in Sunderland, on the afternoon of 13 May 2012, Manchester United had done what they needed to do. Wayne Rooney had headed in Phil Jones's cross after 20 minutes, and United had won 1–0. Everything now depended on Manchester. At the Etihad Stadium, with the 90 minutes up, Manchester City were losing 2–1 to Queens Park Rangers, a team that had played for more than half an hour with ten men. If the scores stayed as they were, the Premier League title would go to Old Trafford.

The scores did not stay as they were. In the second minute of stoppage time Edin Džeko headed in a corner from David Silva. With 93 minutes and 20 seconds on the clock, Sergio Agüero scored again. City were champions of England for the first time in 44 years, and the Sky Sports commentator Martin Tyler's elongated cry of the striker's name, "Agüeroooo", became the most famous call of a long career.

## Eight points behind

In April the title race had looked finished. After a run of poor results City lost 1–0 at Arsenal, leaving them eight points behind United with six matches to play. Then United, top and seemingly in control, faltered. They lost 1–0 at Wigan and drew 4–4 at home to Everton, and on 30 April, with three matches left, the two Manchester clubs met at the Etihad. Vincent Kompany headed the only goal in first-half stoppage time, with Silva supplying the ball. City went top on goal difference.

They kept winning. By the last day the two clubs were level on points, City ahead by eight goals on goal difference, which meant that if City matched United's result they would be champions. City were at home to QPR, who were fighting relegation. United were away at Sunderland.

There was a second plot. City had been bought in 2008 by owners from Abu Dhabi who were spending heavily and expected a place in the top four, and the first manager to pay for not delivering it fast enough was the man now in the away dug-out. QPR's manager was Mark Hughes, whom City's owners had sacked on 19 December 2009, appointing Roberto Mancini in his place. Hughes returned to the Etihad with QPR not yet safe from relegation, against the club that had dismissed him and the manager who had replaced him.

## The afternoon falls apart

For 39 minutes City could not score. Then Pablo Zabaleta put them ahead, and at half-time the title was heading where everyone had expected.

Three minutes after the restart Djibril Cissé equalised for QPR. In the 55th minute QPR's Joey Barton was sent off for an elbow on Carlos Tevez. Before he left the pitch he lashed out at Agüero and tried to head-butt Kompany before his team-mates led him away. The Football Association later found him guilty of two further charges of violent conduct and he was suspended for 12 matches in all: four for the red card and four for each of the two incidents that followed it.

Ten men against the league leaders, at the leaders' ground, should have been the end of QPR's resistance. Instead, in the 66th minute, Jamie Mackie headed them in front. City were losing 2–1. Mancini sent on Džeko and Mario Balotelli to play alongside Agüero, and City laid siege to Paddy Kenny's goal, and Kenny kept them out.

Elsewhere the relegation question was settling itself. Bolton Wanderers, the club below QPR, could only draw 2–2 at Stoke, which meant QPR would stay up whatever happened at the Etihad.

## 93:20

When the 90 minutes ran out, City were still behind. Two minutes later Silva swung over a corner and Džeko rose to head the equaliser. A draw was not enough. City needed another goal, and the remaining seconds of stoppage time were ticking away.

The final move began with Nigel de Jong carrying the ball into the QPR half. He found Agüero about 35 yards from goal. Agüero turned and played the ball in to Balotelli. Balotelli, falling under a challenge, managed to push it back into his path. It was the only assist Balotelli would ever make in the Premier League. Agüero took one touch past the QPR defender Taye Taiwo and struck the ball past Kenny.

The clock read 93:20. Agüero ripped off his shirt and ran. In Sunderland, a title that had been United's since the 66th minute was gone.

Agüero had arrived from Atlético Madrid only ten months earlier, for a reported £38 million, and on his debut, as a substitute against Swansea on 15 August 2011, he had scored twice after coming off the bench. He would go on to become City's record goalscorer. None of the goals that followed was remembered like this one. In an interview published on the fifth anniversary, he described the moment Balotelli, falling, somehow got the ball back to him: it was done, he said, with great skill by a player who was on his way to the ground.

Mancini, who had once seen his team eight points behind, called it, in the various versions the newspapers carried that night, a crazy finish to a crazy season. Both clubs finished on 89 points. City's goal difference was +64, United's +56, and it was the first time a Premier League title had been decided on goal difference.

## The turn

The decisive moment was not the goal but the 66th minute. Going 2–1 down to ten men forced Mancini to throw everything forward, and the substitutions he made, two more strikers alongside Agüero, produced both goals: Džeko scored the first, and Balotelli, in the only Premier League assist of his career, made the second. A team with a weaker nerve, or a manager more afraid of a counter-attack, might have settled for pressure without numbers. Mancini, needing two goals against a side defending with ten men, kept sending players and the ball into the penalty area until the clock ran out or the goals came. They came.

## The 44 years

City's previous league title had also come on the last day of a season. In May 1968 the side managed by Joe Mercer, with Malcolm Allison as his assistant, went to Newcastle level on points with Manchester United at the top of the table, needing to win. They won 4–3, Neil Young scoring twice and Mike Summerbee and Francis Lee getting the others.

In between, City fell a long way. In 1998–99 they spent a season in the third tier for the first time in their history, and they climbed out only through a play-off final at Wembley against Gillingham. Two goals down with the 90 minutes almost gone, City scored through Kevin Horlock and then, deep into stoppage time, through Paul Dickov. Dickov's own kick in the shoot-out hit both posts and stayed out, but the goalkeeper Nicky Weaver saved from Gillingham's Guy Butters and City won it 3–1. It took the club 13 years to get from that afternoon to this one, and both were decided in time added on. For supporters old enough to remember 1968, and young enough to have stood at Wembley in 1999, the afternoon against QPR completed a strange symmetry: the last title and the next one both settled on the final day, and the club's lowest point and its highest both rescued after the 90 minutes were up.

## What it tells us

Seasons are 38 matches long, and they are still decided in moments. City's title was built over months of results and a derby won in April, but it is remembered as 200 seconds in stoppage time, because those seconds held the whole season in them: the collapse, the ten men who would not give in, and the gamble that paid. The lesson for any team chasing something is to keep playing for the goal it needs, rather than the result it fears, until the referee says stop.
