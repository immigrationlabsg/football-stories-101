At 90:00 on 13 May 2012, Manchester City were losing 2–1 at home to Queens Park Rangers.

QPR had been playing with ten men for more than half an hour.

At the Stadium of Light, Manchester United had already won 1–0 at Sunderland.

If the scores stayed as they were, the Premier League title was United's.

And the man in the away dug-out had a history with the home side.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

He was Mark Hughes. City's Abu Dhabi owners had sacked him on 19 December 2009 and appointed Roberto Mancini in his place.

So Hughes came back to the Etihad with QPR not yet safe, facing the club that had dismissed him and the manager who had replaced him.

City had been written off once already that spring. In April they lost 1–0 at Arsenal and fell eight points behind United with six matches to play.

Then United wobbled. They lost 1–0 at Wigan and drew 4–4 at home to Everton.

On 30 April Vincent Kompany headed the only goal of the Manchester derby, and City went top on goal difference.

By the last day the clubs were level on points. If City matched United's result, they were champions.

At home. Against a side fighting relegation.

In Sunderland, Wayne Rooney headed in Phil Jones's cross after 20 minutes.

At the Etihad, City could not score for 39 minutes. Then Pablo Zabaleta put them ahead.

Three minutes after half-time Djibril Cissé equalised.

In the 55th minute QPR's Joey Barton was sent off for an elbow on Carlos Tevez. Before he left the pitch he lashed out at Sergio Agüero and tried to head-butt Kompany.

The Football Association later suspended him for 12 matches in all.

Ten men, away at the leaders. It should have been over.

Then came the part that surprised me.

In the 66th minute Jamie Mackie headed QPR in front.

Mancini sent on Edin Džeko and Mario Balotelli to play alongside Agüero. City laid siege to Paddy Kenny's goal, and Kenny kept them out.

Elsewhere, Bolton Wanderers could only draw 2–2 at Stoke. QPR were safe whatever happened.

When the 90 minutes ran out, City were still behind.

Two minutes into stoppage time, David Silva swung over a corner and Džeko headed the equaliser.

A draw was no use. City needed another goal.

Nigel de Jong carried the ball into the QPR half and found Agüero about 35 yards out. Agüero turned and played it in to Balotelli.

Balotelli, falling under a challenge, pushed it back into his path. It was the only assist Balotelli ever made in the Premier League.

Agüero took one touch past Taye Taiwo and struck the ball past Kenny.

The clock read 93:20.

Agüero ripped off his shirt and ran. On Sky Sports, Martin Tyler's stretched-out cry of "Agüeroooo" became the most famous call of a long career.

In Sunderland, a title that had been United's since the 66th minute was gone.

Both clubs finished on 89 points. City's goal difference was +64, United's +56. It was the first Premier League title decided on goal difference.

City were champions of England for the first time in 44 years.

And the 44 years have a strange shape.

Their previous title, in May 1968, had also come on the final day. Joe Mercer's side went to Newcastle level on points with United, needing to win, and won 4–3.

In between, City fell into the third tier for the first time in their history. In 1999 they climbed out through a play-off final at Wembley against Gillingham.

They were two goals down with the 90 minutes almost gone. Kevin Horlock scored. Then Paul Dickov scored, deep into stoppage time. City won the shoot-out 3–1.

The club's lowest point and its highest, both rescued after the 90 minutes were up.

Here's what stays with me.

A season is 38 matches long, and this one is remembered as 200 seconds. Both goals came from strikers sent on in desperation, when caution would have looked sensible. Sometimes the bravest thing a team can do is keep playing for the goal it needs, not the result it fears.
