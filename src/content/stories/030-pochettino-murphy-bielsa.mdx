Mauricio Pochettino's first game as Manchester United's head coach is on Sunday, at Liverpool.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

His story began at 1am, on a winter night, in a small farming town in Argentina.

The town was Murphy, in the south of Santa Fe province. About 4,000 people live there, and the town lives off the land.

His family farmed. His first work was on the farms.

That night two football coaches from Newell's Old Boys drove to Murphy. One was Marcelo Bielsa. The other was Jorge Griffa.

Pochettino remembers the streets empty except for stray dogs.

They knocked on the Pochettino family's door. His parents were frightened, he has said.

The boy they had come for was asleep.

His family let them see him anyway. One of the two men looked at the sleeping boy and said he had a footballer's legs.

That was the visit. It was not yet a place at the club.

Then came the part that surprised me.

There was still a test. At his first session in Rosario, Pochettino says, Bielsa told him he would play up front. His scouts had him down as a striker. Back home he had been scoring as a makeshift centre-forward for his local club.

The teenager said no.

As Pochettino tells it, his answer was: "No, I am a centre-back."

Bielsa let him play there. Within minutes, Pochettino says, Bielsa told him he would be a Newell's Old Boys player.

So the first thing he did in front of Bielsa was say no to him, and he got his way.

Now that centre-back is Manchester United's head coach, from 18 November.

On Sunday he takes his new team to Anfield.

Here's what stays with me.

Two coaches drove to a small town in the middle of the night to look at a sleeping boy's legs.

The boy they woke up already knew where he wanted to play.

Not bad for a boy who said no to Bielsa.
