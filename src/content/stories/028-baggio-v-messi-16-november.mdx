Lionel Messi's first game for Argentina was over almost as soon as it began.

Budapest, 17 August 2005. He was 18, and he came off the bench against Hungary in the second half, for Lisandro López.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Hungary's defender Vilmos Vanczák grabbed his shirt. Messi's arm swung free as he pulled away.

The referee, Markus Merk of Germany, showed him a straight red.

His coach, José Pékerman, has said Messi was in tears.

Argentina still won the friendly 2–1. Their new teenager had lasted less than a minute.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Years later, Merk admitted a yellow card would also have been fair.

Even Vanczák said Messi didn't deserve the red, and that he felt sorry for him.

Messi was shy off the pitch. But he has said he hated losing ever since he was a little boy.

He became Argentina's most-capped player, and their captain. He won the Ballon d'Or eight times.

But the big one kept slipping away.

In 2014 Argentina reached the World Cup final. They lost 1–0 to Germany, after extra time.

In 2016 the Copa América final against Chile went to penalties. Messi took Argentina's first and sent it over the bar. Chile won the shootout 4–2.

Afterwards, he said he was quitting Argentina. "It hurts not to be a champion," he told reporters.

He came back that August.

And in 2022, in Qatar, he scored twice in the World Cup final against France. Argentina won on penalties. He was a world champion.

Here's what stays with me.

His first game for his country lasted less than a minute, and the referee later said a yellow would also have been fair.

Even the defender felt sorry for him.

He nearly walked away from Argentina once. He came back, and the big one came too.

Not bad for a career that began with a red card.
