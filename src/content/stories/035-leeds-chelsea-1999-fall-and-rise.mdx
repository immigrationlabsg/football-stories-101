Leeds United had not won at Stamford Bridge since December 1999. The night they last did, they went top of the league. Then the club nearly died.

On Sunday 19 December 1999, David O'Leary's side came to Chelsea young and stacked: Harry Kewell, Lee Bowyer, Jonathan Woodgate, and Lucas Radebe as captain. The goals came from Stephen McPhail, a 20-year-old from the club's academy, who scored in the 66th and 87th minutes. Leeds won 2-0 and were top of the league at Christmas.

The next November they signed Rio Ferdinand from West Ham for £18m, the most anyone had paid for a defender. In 2001 they reached the Champions League semi-final, and lost 3-0 in Valencia.

Here is the part that is easy to forget. The club had borrowed against Champions League money it expected to keep earning. It did not qualify again. Ferdinand went to Manchester United in 2002, Woodgate to Newcastle in January 2003 and Kewell to Liverpool that summer. In 2004 Leeds were relegated.

By 2007 they had gone into administration and dropped into the third tier. Before the season began, the Football League docked them 15 points, so they started League One on minus 15.

It took until 2020, and 16 years away, to get back to the Premier League. Three years later they went down again, losing 4-1 at home to Tottenham on the final day. Daniel Farke brought them back up as champions in 2025.

This year Chelsea knocked them out of two cups. In April, Enzo Fernández's header won the FA Cup semi-final 1-0 at Wembley. In September, in the League Cup at Stamford Bridge, Leeds led 2-0 and lost 6-3.

Then came Saturday. Emegha put Chelsea ahead. Two minutes and fifty-one seconds later Noah Okafor equalised. Dominic Calvert-Lewin scored a penalty, Okafor got his second and Brenden Aaronson added a fourth in stoppage time. Chelsea 1, Leeds 4.

It was Leeds' first win at Stamford Bridge since McPhail's night in 1999, and it lifted them to sixth, above Chelsea in eighth.

Leeds: back for good, or 1999 all over again?
