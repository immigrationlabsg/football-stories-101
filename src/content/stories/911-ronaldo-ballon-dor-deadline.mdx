Solna, 19 November 2013. Portugal played Zlatan Ibrahimović's Sweden in the second leg of a World Cup play-off.

Cristiano Ronaldo had scored the only goal of the first leg, in Lisbon, four days earlier.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

That first leg, on 15 November, was also the day voting for the 2013 Ballon d'Or was due to close.

In Solna, Ronaldo scored five minutes after half-time. Ibrahimović then scored twice, and Sweden led 2–1 on the night.

Ronaldo answered with two more goals. Portugal won 3–2, went through 4–2 on aggregate, and went to the World Cup in Brazil.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Under the original deadline, those three goals came too late for the vote.

But FIFA moved the deadline to 29 November. It said too few voters had replied. Now the hat-trick could be taken into account.

Lionel Messi had won the previous four Ballons d'Or.

On 13 January 2014, in Zurich, Ronaldo won it for the second time, and Messi's run was over.

Here's what stays with me.

Three goals arrived after the vote should have closed.

Two months later, the prize was his.

13 years on, people remember the hat-trick. Fewer remember that the deadline moved.
