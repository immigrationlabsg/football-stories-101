In stoppage time at Old Trafford in September 2003, Ruud van Nistelrooy stood over a penalty. It was 0–0.

Arsenal were down to ten men. Their captain, Patrick Vieira, had been sent off.

Van Nistelrooy hit the crossbar.

That miss saved the most famous unbeaten season in English football.

Here’s what I didn’t know until recently.

A year earlier, Arsène Wenger had been laughed at for predicting it.

In 2002 he said Arsenal could go a league season unbeaten. No top-flight side had done it since Preston North End in 1888–89.

Within weeks it was a joke. At Goodison Park, Everton’s Wayne Rooney, five days short of 17, scored a late winner.

Manchester United took the title. Somebody threw Wenger a T-shirt mocking his prediction.

As he put it in 2004: “I was just a season too early.”

When it came, the season was not built on winning. Arsenal drew 12 of their 38 games.

Thierry Henry scored 30 league goals.

The test came in one week of April 2004.

Manchester United beat them in the FA Cup semi-final. Three days later Chelsea knocked them out of the Champions League.

Three trophies had become one.

Then came the part that surprised me.

On Good Friday, Liverpool came to Highbury and led 2–1 at half-time.

Arsenal won 4–2. Henry scored a hat-trick.

They did not lose again.

On the final day, relegated Leicester City led at Highbury at half-time. The season was 45 minutes from being lost.

Henry scored a penalty. Vieira added the winner from a Dennis Bergkamp pass.

Played 38, won 26, drawn 12, lost none.

The run reached 49 league games. It ended at Old Trafford in October 2004: a van Nistelrooy penalty, and a goal from Rooney, by then a United player.

Van Nistelrooy and Rooney, again.

Here’s what stays with me.

The Invincibles are remembered for Henry’s brilliance. Their record rests on something duller: refusing to lose the games they were not winning.

A prediction that was laughed at became the standard.
