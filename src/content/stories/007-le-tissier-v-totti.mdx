On 26 October 1996, Matt Le Tissier chipped Peter Schmeichel at The Dell. Southampton beat Manchester United 6–3.

On 26 October 2005, Francesco Totti picked up the ball in his own half at San Siro, skipped past Esteban Cambiasso and saw Júlio César off his line.

He chipped him too. Roma, already ahead through Vincenzo Montella, won 3–2.

Same date. Same shot. Nine years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The two men shared far more than one finish.

Le Tissier spent his whole professional career at Southampton, from 1986 to 2002.

Tottenham, the team he supported as a boy, tried to sign him. Chelsea wanted him too. He stayed, and Southampton's fans called him Le God.

He became the first midfielder to score 100 Premier League goals. He took 48 penalties in his career and scored 47.

Totti spent his whole career at Roma: 25 seasons. In 2004 he came close to joining Real Madrid. He stayed.

He scored 307 goals for Roma. Italians even have a word for his favourite finish: the cucchiaio, the spoon.

Totti later called the chip at San Siro one of the best two or three goals of his career.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Staying cost them very different amounts.

Totti won Serie A in 2001 and the World Cup with Italy in 2006.

Le Tissier never won a major trophy. He played 8 times for England and never scored.

Same gift. Same loyalty. Very different endings.

Here's what stays with me.

A chip is a shot you only try when you are certain.

Both men were that certain about the ball, and about where they belonged.

One is remembered with medals. The other only with love.
