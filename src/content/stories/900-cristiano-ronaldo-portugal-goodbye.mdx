On Thursday, Cristiano Ronaldo handed back Portugal's No. 7 shirt.

He is 41. He leaves with 146 goals in 234 games, both men's international records.

Everyone has those numbers.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Before he arrived, Portugal had played at three World Cups and three European Championships in their entire history. They had never won a major trophy.

He came on for Luís Figo against Kazakhstan in August 2003. From then on, they never missed a tournament.

At Euro 2004, at home, he was 19. Portugal lost the final to Greece in Lisbon, and he cried on the pitch.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Months later, the tsunami hit Aceh in Indonesia. A 7-year-old called Martunis was found about three weeks afterwards, alone, wearing a Portugal shirt.

In 2005, Ronaldo travelled to Aceh and met him. In 2015, Sporting signed Martunis to their academy in Portugal.

The trophy took twelve years from Lisbon.

In the Euro 2016 final against France, Ronaldo was carried off injured in the first half, in tears. He came back to stand on the touchline in extra time. Éder scored. Portugal won 1–0.

Two Nations League titles followed.

His last game was a 1–0 win over Wales in September. Days later he left the camp after a public disagreement with coach Jorge Jesus over his role, and the federation suspended him for 30 days. He apologised. Jesus said he would be welcome back. He chose to go instead. His goodbye came in a video, not in a stadium. The federation says a tribute will follow.

"The No. 7 shirt will remain," he said. "The pride of having worn it will be mine forever."

Here's what stays with me.

Records get beaten. That is what records are for.

But a national team that had never won a major trophy now expects to. Nobody can beat that, because it only had to happen once.

Obrigado, Cristiano.
