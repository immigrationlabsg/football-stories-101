Paris, 18 November 2009. France and the Republic of Ireland went to extra time in a World Cup play-off.

Thierry Henry handled the ball twice, crossed, and William Gallas scored. France went through, 2–1 on aggregate.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Ireland were out, but the story did not end at the final whistle.

Two days later, Henry said the fairest solution would be to replay the game. FIFA said no replay would be granted.

So Ireland made a quieter request. Could they be team number 33 at the finals?

Sepp Blatter revealed it himself, at the Soccerex conference in Johannesburg later that month. His audience laughed.

Then came the part that surprised me.

In January 2010, FIFA paid Ireland's football association, the FAI, €5 million to put an end to any claims against it. FIFA's first statement gave the sum in dollars; it later confirmed €5 million.

The FAI's chief executive, John Delaney, only confirmed the payment publicly in June 2015, on Irish radio.

FIFA called the money a loan, to be repaid if Ireland qualified for the 2014 World Cup.

They did not qualify. FIFA wrote the loan off as of 31 December 2014.

And France? At the 2010 World Cup they drew 0–0 with Uruguay, lost 2–0 to Mexico and lost 2–1 to South Africa.

One point from three games, and out in the group stage.

Here's what stays with me.

One hand decided a place at a World Cup. FIFA would not replay the game, yet it paid.

And the team that went through did not win a single match there.

17 years on, everyone remembers the hand. Far fewer people ever saw the money.
