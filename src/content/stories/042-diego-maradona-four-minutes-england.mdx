Six minutes into the second half at the Estadio Azteca, on 22 June 1986, an England midfielder miscued a back-pass.

The ball rose. Diego Maradona and Peter Shilton went up for it together.

Shilton was much the taller. So Maradona raised his fist and punched the ball into the net.

Four minutes later he scored again. The two goals have argued over him ever since.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The famous line may never have been said.

The story goes that Maradona said the goal was scored a little with his head and a little with the hand of God.

But as La Nación has pointed out, there is no film or audio of him saying it. Later in 1986, Maradona wrote that it was completely false.

It became his anyway.

The hand itself is not in doubt. In 2005, on his own television show, he said he had punched it because he could not head it past Shilton. He did not regret it for a second.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The second goal began in Argentina’s own half. Over roughly 60 yards and about 11 seconds, Maradona went past five England players and then round Shilton.

Jorge Valdano, his team-mate, later said Maradona had been looking to pass to him the whole way.

He never found the moment. So he kept going.

Six years earlier, at Wembley, he had run through the England defence, taken on the goalkeeper Ray Clemence and shot wide.

Afterwards his younger brother Hugo told him he should have gone round the keeper.

In 1986, faced with Shilton, he remembered.

Gary Lineker headed one back. Argentina held on, 2–1, in front of 114,580 people.

After the match Steve Hodge, whose miscued ball had set up the first goal, swapped shirts with Maradona. In May 2022 it sold at Sotheby’s for £7,142,500.

Here's what stays with me.

The cheat and the genius are the same man, four minutes apart, and nobody gets to choose one. And the most famous words in the story may never have been spoken at all.
