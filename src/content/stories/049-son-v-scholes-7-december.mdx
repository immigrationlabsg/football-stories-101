On 7 December 2002, Manchester United beat Arsenal 2–0 at Old Trafford. Juan Sebastián Verón scored the first. Paul Scholes, set up by Ruud van Nistelrooy, added the second with 17 minutes left.

On 7 December 2019, Son Heung-min picked up the ball on the edge of his own penalty area against Burnley. He ran the length of the pitch, past one Burnley player after another, and beat Nick Pope.

Two runs. Same date. Seventeen years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

That 2–0 ended a run of its own. Arsenal had scored in 55 Premier League games in a row. At Old Trafford, they did not score at all.

The run began on the last day of the 2000–01 season, with an Ashley Cole goal in a 3–2 defeat at Southampton.

Arsenal then scored in all 38 of their league games in 2001–02. They clinched the title at Old Trafford, through Sylvain Wiltord's goal on 8 May 2002.

Seven months later they went back with Thierry Henry, Patrick Vieira, Robert Pires and Freddie Ljungberg in the side. Dennis Bergkamp came off the bench.

United were without David Beckham, Roy Keane and Rio Ferdinand. Arsenal still could not find a goal.

Then came the part that surprised me.

A run that began with a defeat ended with one, 18 months later. It was a Premier League record.

Son's run was over in about 11 seconds.

Afterwards José Mourinho, in his third week as Tottenham manager, compared it with Ronaldo's solo goal for Barcelona against Compostela in 1996. Mourinho had watched that one sitting beside Bobby Robson.

Son's goal was voted the Premier League's Goal of the Season. In December 2020, it won FIFA's Puskás Award, ahead of Luis Suárez and Giorgian de Arrascaeta.

Here's what stays with me.

One run lasted about 11 seconds. The other lasted 18 months.

Arsenal's ended on the ground where they had clinched the title.

Son's began on the edge of his own penalty area.
