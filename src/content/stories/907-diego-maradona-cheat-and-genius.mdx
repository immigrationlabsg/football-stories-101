At the Azteca on 22 June 1986, Diego Maradona and Peter Shilton went up for the same ball.

Shilton was much the taller. So Maradona punched it into the net.

Four minutes later he scored the Goal of the Century.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

That second goal began with a miss, six years earlier.

At Wembley in 1980, he ran through the England defence, took on the goalkeeper Ray Clemence and shot wide.

Afterwards his younger brother Hugo told him he should have gone round the keeper.

Then came the part that surprised me.

In 1986 he picked up the ball in his own half. Over roughly 60 yards and about 11 seconds, he went past five England players.

Then he came to Shilton. And he remembered.

He went round him.

Argentina won 2–1, in front of 114,580 people, and went on to win the World Cup.

In 2005, on his own television show, he said he had punched the first one because he could not head it past Shilton. He did not regret it for a second.

Here's what stays with me.

Nobody gets to choose one of those goals. The cheat and the genius are the same man, four minutes apart.
