On 4 November, Como won 9–1 at Lens in the Champions League.

UEFA says it equals the biggest margin of victory in the competition's history.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Nine years ago, this club went bust.

It was the second bankruptcy in thirteen years. Como, a small club on a lake in northern Italy, fell to Serie D, the fourth division, and started again.

It was still down there in 2019, when two Indonesian billionaire brothers bought it. Reports of the price differ, but they all come in under a million euros.

Then came the part that surprised me.

In 2022, with Como in the second division, Cesc Fàbregas signed for them as a player and took a minority stake in the club. In late 2023 he took over as caretaker coach.

In May 2024 they went up to Serie A, for the first time in 21 years. That summer he became the manager.

Last season they finished fourth. It put a club founded in 1907 into its first ever Champions League, 119 years on.

And this month, in that first campaign, they scored nine in France.

Here's what stays with me.

It took this club 119 years to reach the Champions League, and only nine to get there from the bottom.

Nine years. Nine goals.
