On 10 December 2011, Karim Benzema scored against Barcelona at the Bernabéu before the Clásico was half a minute old.

Barcelona's goalkeeper, Víctor Valdés, miscued a clearance. Reports timed Benzema's finish at between 21 and 23 seconds.

Real Madrid lost 3–1. Alexis Sánchez, Xavi and Cesc Fàbregas scored for Barcelona.

On 10 December 2017, Mohamed Salah curled a shot into the far corner against Everton at Anfield, just before half-time.

Liverpool drew 1–1. Wayne Rooney equalised from the penalty spot with about a quarter of an hour left. It was his first goal in a Merseyside derby.

Two openers. Same date. Neither won.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Benzema's night was not a sign of things to come.

The defeat ended a run of 15 straight wins for Real Madrid in all competitions, and Barcelona went top on goal difference.

Real lost only one other league game all season. They finished with 100 points, the first Spanish side to reach a century, nine clear of Barcelona. They scored 121 league goals.

Salah's season went the other way round.

His goal against Everton was one of 32 he scored in the league that season, a Premier League record for a 38-game campaign. The old mark of 31 was shared by Alan Shearer, Cristiano Ronaldo and Luis Suárez.

Liverpool finished fourth.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Five months later, the two men met in the Champions League final in Kyiv.

Salah went off injured in the first half.

Benzema opened the scoring after Liverpool's goalkeeper, Loris Karius, threw the ball straight into his leg.

Real Madrid won 3–1. The same score as the Clásico, with Benzema on the right side of it this time.

Both of those Benzema goals began with a goalkeeper's mistake.

Here's what stays with me.

A goal is a promise, not a result.

Benzema's promise was broken that night and kept by May.

Salah's record season ended in the first half of the final.
