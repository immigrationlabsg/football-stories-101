In December 1960, Benfica flew an 18-year-old from Mozambique to Lisbon and gave him a codename: Ruth.

Then they hid him, in a hotel in a fishing town in the Algarve.

The boy was Eusébio da Silva Ferreira. Five years later he won the Ballon d'Or.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Another club was sure he was already theirs.

Eusébio was born in 1942 in Mafalala, a poor district of Lourenço Marques, now Maputo.

At 15 he joined Sporting Clube de Lourenço Marques, a nursery club of Sporting in Lisbon. Sporting assumed he would one day be theirs.

José Carlos Bauer, a former Brazil international, had seen him in Mozambique. At a Lisbon barber's he told his old coach Béla Guttmann, by then in charge of Benfica.

Guttmann moved fast. Benfica reached an agreement with the family while Sporting were still assuming.

The fishing town where they hid him was Lagos.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Before the 1962 European Cup final against Real Madrid, the 20-year-old asked a team-mate to introduce him to his idol, Alfredo Di Stéfano.

And to ask for his shirt after the match.

Ferenc Puskás scored three in the first half. Then Eusébio scored twice in about five minutes. Benfica won 5–3.

Di Stéfano kept his promise. Eusébio later said he cared more about the shirt than the cup.

Then came the 1966 World Cup.

By the 25th minute of the quarter-final, Portugal were 3–0 down to North Korea.

Two minutes later Eusébio scored. He did not celebrate. He took the ball out of the net, ran it back and put it on the centre spot.

He scored four in about half an hour. Portugal won 5–3.

He finished with nine goals, the most at the tournament.

Here's what stays with me.

One of football's greatest careers turned on a chat in a barber's shop. He belongs to Mozambique and to Portugal at once.

And the boy who could take apart any defence still wanted his hero's shirt more than the trophy.
