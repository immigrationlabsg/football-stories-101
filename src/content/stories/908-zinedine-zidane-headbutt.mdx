A headbutt. That is how Zinedine Zidane ended his career, in extra time of a World Cup final.

Twenty years on he coaches France. They played Italy in Milan this week and meet Turkey in Bordeaux today.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

What was said depends on who you ask.

Both men agree it began with a shirt. Materazzi had hold of it, and Zidane told him he could have it after the match. Materazzi admits he answered with an insult about Zidane's sister. He says he never mentioned Zidane's mother, because he lost his own at 15. He says he did not start it, and that his words didn't warrant the reaction.

Zidane says the words were about his mother and his sister, and that they came again and again. He says he walked away, heard them once more, and went back.

His mother was ill in hospital at the time. "It is not an excuse," Zidane said in 2010. Materazzi says he didn't know.

Materazzi denies saying anything about race, religion or politics, and FIFA said both players told it the words were not racist. It banned them both: three matches for Zidane, two for Materazzi.

In 2006 Zidane called his act "not forgivable" and asked forgiveness from the children who watched. He also said: "I don't regret my behaviour." Regret, he said, would mean Materazzi was right. One person did not get an apology. "I'd rather die," he said of Materazzi in 2010.

France lost on penalties without him. He still won the Golden Ball. Most votes, the BBC reported, were cast at half-time, before the red card.

And it sits beside everything else. Two headers in the 1998 final. The volley in Glasgow in 2002. Then three straight Champions Leagues as Real Madrid's coach.

He called it not forgivable. He just would not say sorry to the man.
