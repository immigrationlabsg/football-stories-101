A headbutt, minutes from the end of Zinedine Zidane's career, in a World Cup final. What did Marco Materazzi say?

Twenty years on, Zidane coaches France, and they faced Italy again, drawing 1–1 in October.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Materazzi told the story himself.

Berlin, 2006, extra time, 1–1. He was tugging Zidane's shirt. Zidane told him he could have it afterwards. In September 2006 Materazzi told Gazzetta dello Sport his answer: "I would prefer your sister."

He says he never mentioned Zidane's mother, because he lost his own at 15.

Zidane says the words were about his mother and his sister. He says he walked away, heard them again, and went back.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Zidane said in 2010 that his mother was ill in hospital at the time. Materazzi says he didn't know.

Zidane was sent off in the 110th minute. France lost on penalties.

Materazzi denies saying a word about race, religion or politics, and FIFA said both players told it the words were not racist. It banned Materazzi for two matches and Zidane for three. His ban was symbolic, as he had retired.

He had by then asked forgiveness from the children who watched. In 2006 he also said: "I don't regret my behaviour." By 2010 it was: "Of course I reproach myself." Still he would not say sorry to the man. "I'd rather die," he said that year.

Here's what stays with me.

It took nothing away. He still won the Golden Ball, though the BBC reported most votes were cast at half-time. Two headers in the 1998 final. The volley in Glasgow in 2002. Then three straight Champions Leagues as coach of Real Madrid.

In 2010 he reproached himself and still wouldn't say sorry. The career stands.
