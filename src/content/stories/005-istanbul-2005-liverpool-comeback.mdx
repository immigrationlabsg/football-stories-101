Inside the first minute in Istanbul, on 25 May 2005, Paolo Maldini volleyed in a free-kick.

No Champions League final has had an earlier goal.

Hernán Crespo scored twice more before the break. At half-time Milan led Liverpool 3–0.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

What happened in the dressing room was not a speech.

It was a muddle about a substitution.

Jamie Carragher admitted he did not think the game could be turned round.

But Rafael Benítez had already decided the shape was wrong. Kaká was running through the space in front of the back four.

The fix was Dietmar Hamann. Bring him on to sit on Kaká, push Steven Gerrard forward.

The player due to make way was the left-back, Djimi Traoré.

Then the physio said the right-back, Steve Finnan, could not carry on. So Finnan came off instead, and Traoré was sent back out.

In the confusion, Liverpool nearly sent out 12 men.

Through the walls, the players could hear the travelling supporters singing "You'll Never Walk Alone" to a team 3–0 down.

The first goal came on 54 minutes. Gerrard, now much higher up the pitch, headed in a cross.

Two minutes later Vladimír Šmicer scored from outside the area.

On the hour Gerrard was brought down. Penalty.

But here's where it gets interesting.

Xabi Alonso took it, and he later said he had never taken a penalty as a professional.

Dida saved it. Alonso scored the rebound.

Three goals in six minutes. 3–3.

Traoré, the left-back who was supposed to be sitting in the dressing room, cleared a shot from Andriy Shevchenko off the line.

In the shoot-out Jerzy Dudek saved from Andrea Pirlo. Then, with Milan needing to score, he saved from Shevchenko.

Liverpool won 3–2 on penalties.

Here's what stays with me.

This is remembered as a triumph of spirit, and it was.

But it rested on a tactical change that was right, and a substitution that went wrong in a useful way.

Great nights are often less a plan than a run of lucky corrections.
