On Thursday 11 April 2024, a river barge called the Gabarra moved slowly up the Nervión estuary towards the city hall of Bilbao.

On its deck stood the players of Athletic Club, holding the Copa del Rey. It was the club's first major trophy in 40 years.

Every player on that barge had been born or brought up in the Basque Country, or had learned his football at a Basque club.

It is a rule the club has lived by for more than a century.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The rule has never been written down. It is a custom, and it has held.

It began with a row. In the 1911 Copa del Rey, rivals protested that Athletic were fielding English players who were not eligible. The next year the federation required all players to be Spanish citizens.

Accounts differ on when Athletic went further and picked locals only; sources give dates from 1911 to 1919.

Even the colours came from England, at least in the club's version. In late 1909 a member named Juan Elorduy was sent to buy Blackburn Rovers kits. He could not find enough, and came home with red-and-white stripes from Southampton.

Some historians doubt it. The club keeps the Southampton story. The spare shirts, it says, went to its Madrid branch, which grew into Atlético Madrid.

Coaches are exempt. The most celebrated was an Englishman, Fred Pentland, known as El Bombín, the bowler hat. After a trophy, his players took the hat from him and smashed it.

It became a tradition.

By any modern logic the rule should have sunk the club.

It has not. Athletic have played every season of La Liga since it began in 1929 and have never been relegated. Only Real Madrid and Barcelona share that record.

Telmo Zarra scored 251 league goals for them. His total stood as the Spanish record until Lionel Messi passed it in November 2014.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The rule defines "our own" by where a footballer was formed, not by where his family came from. That is what put Iñaki Williams on the barge.

His parents left Ghana and crossed the Sahara to reach Spain. His father has described walking the desert barefoot.

In Bilbao a Catholic priest, Iñaki Mardones, helped them find a home and saw them through the birth of their first son.

They named the boy after him.

Iñaki Williams was born in Bilbao, grew up in Navarre and came through Athletic's academy. For years he said he felt Basque, before choosing to play for Ghana in 2022.

On 20 April 2016 he came on against Atlético Madrid. He did not miss another La Liga match for almost seven years.

When the run ended in January 2023, it had reached 251 consecutive league games, a league record.

His younger brother, Nico, was one of the stars of the cup-winning side.

That cup had been a long time coming. On the eve of the 2024 final against Mallorca, the coach, Ernesto Valverde, was frank. As he put it: "We always have to try to win, but we have played five finals this century and we have lost all of them."

In Seville, Mallorca led at half-time. Oihan Sancet equalised five minutes after the break, and nobody scored again in 120 minutes.

In the shoot-out the goalkeeper Julen Agirrezabala saved one Mallorca penalty, another was missed, and Alex Berenguer struck the winner. Athletic took it 4–2.

The Gabarra only sails when Athletic win something big. When they won the league in 1983, a club director decided the usual balcony appearance would not do, and proposed a working barge instead.

Then it was retired to the city's maritime museum. It had not been out in 40 years.

This time the banks, bridges and balconies turned red and white. Agency reporters counted hundreds of thousands; local estimates ran to a million.

Here's what stays with me.

A rule that began as a way of keeping foreign professionals out has become a way of letting a city's newest families in. The club never changed the rule. The city changed, and the rule followed it.
