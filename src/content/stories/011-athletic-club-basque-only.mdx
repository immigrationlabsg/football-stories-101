In April 2024, a river barge called the Gabarra sailed into Bilbao.

On deck stood Athletic Club's players, holding the Copa del Rey, their first major trophy in 40 years.

Every one of them had been born or raised in the Basque Country, or learned his football at a Basque club.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

That rule has never been written down. It has simply held for more than a century.

By modern logic it should have sunk the club. Yet Athletic have played every La Liga season since 1929 and never been relegated. Only Real Madrid and Barcelona can say the same.

Even the colours were borrowed, in the club's version: Southampton shirts bought in England in 1909. Some historians doubt it. The spares, the club says, went to its Madrid branch, which grew into Atlético Madrid.

Telmo Zarra's 251 league goals for Athletic stood as the Spanish record until Lionel Messi passed it in 2014.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The rule counts where a footballer was formed, not where his family came from. That is what put Iñaki Williams on the barge.

His Ghanaian parents crossed the Sahara, his father barefoot.

In Bilbao a priest, Iñaki Mardones, helped them find a home. They named their first son after him.

Iñaki, born in Bilbao, came through Athletic's academy. From April 2016 he did not miss a La Liga match for almost seven years: 251 in a row, a league record.

His younger brother, Nico, was a star of the cup-winning side.

In the final, Mallorca led at half-time. Oihan Sancet equalised, and Alex Berenguer struck the winning penalty in a 4–2 shoot-out.

The Gabarra only sails for big wins. It had not been out in 40 years. Agency reporters counted hundreds of thousands; local estimates ran to a million.

Here's what stays with me. A rule that began as a way of keeping foreign professionals out now lets a city's newest families in. The club never changed the rule. The city changed, and the rule followed it.
