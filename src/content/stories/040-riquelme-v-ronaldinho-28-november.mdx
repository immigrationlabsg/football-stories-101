On 28 November 2000, Boca Juniors met Real Madrid, the champions of Europe, in Tokyo.

Martín Palermo scored twice in the first 6 minutes, one of them from a pass of about 50 metres by Juan Román Riquelme. Roberto Carlos pulled one back. Boca won 2–1.

Riquelme ran the game. Claude Makélélé spent the night chasing him.

On 28 November 2005, in Paris, Ronaldinho won the Ballon d'Or. Frank Lampard was second, Steven Gerrard third.

Two playmakers. Same date. Five years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Barcelona had them both, one after the other.

In 2002 Barcelona signed Riquelme from Boca and gave him the number 10 shirt. The coach, Louis van Gaal, often played him on the wing.

In the summer of 2003 Barcelona signed Ronaldinho from Paris Saint-Germain. He took the 10.

Spanish rules let a club register only four players from outside the European Union. Ronaldinho's arrival left Barcelona with one too many. Riquelme was sent on loan to Villarreal.

Then came the part that surprised me.

In 2006 Villarreal, with Riquelme, reached the semi-finals of the Champions League.

Against Arsenal, in the last minutes of the second leg, Riquelme took a penalty that would have forced extra time. Jens Lehmann saved it.

Arsenal went through to the final in Paris. Their opponents were Barcelona. Barcelona won 2–1, and Ronaldinho had his Champions League.

In 2007 Riquelme went back to Boca on loan. He scored twice in the second leg of the final against Grêmio and won the Copa Libertadores for the third time.

Ronaldinho won it in 2013, with Atlético Mineiro.

And the shirt Riquelme wore in Tokyo? Luís Figo swapped for it that night, kept it for 23 years, then pledged it to a football museum in Madrid.

Here's what stays with me.

A club can give its 10 to only one player at a time.

Barcelona chose Ronaldinho, and won the Champions League.

Riquelme keeps Tokyo, and the night he outplayed the champions of Europe.
