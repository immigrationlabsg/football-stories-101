On 22 November 2009, Jermain Defoe scored five goals as Tottenham beat Wigan 9–1 at White Hart Lane.

On 22 November 2022, Olivier Giroud scored twice as France beat Australia 4–1 at the World Cup.

His second was his 51st goal for France. It put him level with Thierry Henry.

Same date. Two strikers. Two records matched, not broken.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

That Tottenham afternoon did not start as a rout. Spurs led only 1–0 at half-time, through a Peter Crouch header.

Then Defoe scored three times in seven minutes, after 51, 54 and 58 minutes. He added two more before the end.

His five goals equalled the Premier League record for one player in a match. Andy Cole had done it against Ipswich, and Alan Shearer against Sheffield Wednesday in 1999. Nobody had ever scored six.

Afterwards, Wigan's players offered to refund their supporters' tickets.

France arrived as world champions, and their night started badly too. Craig Goodwin gave Australia the lead. Adrien Rabiot equalised, and Giroud tapped in to make it 2–1.

His second, the record-equaller, was a header from a Kylian Mbappé cross, 19 minutes from time.

Henry had scored his 51 in 123 games for France, between 1997 and 2010.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Giroud was only starting because of somebody else's misfortune. Karim Benzema had been ruled out injured before the tournament, and that opened the door.

Giroud was 36. Nobody older had ever scored for France at a World Cup.

So a striker who might not have started at all ended the night level with his country's record scorer.

Here's what stays with me.

Neither man broke a record that day. Both drew level with famous names.

One record was made in a single afternoon, three of the goals in seven minutes. The other took a whole international career.

A shared record is still a record, and nobody can take the day away.
