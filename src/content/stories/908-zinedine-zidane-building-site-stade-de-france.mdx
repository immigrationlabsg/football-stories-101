This weekend, Zinedine Zidane's France play Turkey in Bordeaux. He was named their coach in July.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

His greatest night started with a man sleeping on a building site.

The man was his father, Smail Zidane, who came from Algeria as a young man and found work as a labourer in Paris.

In the winter of 1954 he could not pay for a room. So he stayed behind on the site and slept in a corner of an unfinished flat.

He told this himself in his autobiography. He says the site, at the Porte de Clignancourt, was a stone's throw from the future Stade de France.

Then came Marseille and La Castellane, a rough housing estate, where Smail worked nights as a watchman. The youngest of his five children was Zinedine.

On 12 July 1998, Zinedine played in the World Cup final against Brazil, at the Stade de France. He scored two headers before half-time. France won 3–0.

Smail did not watch.

By several accounts he could not bear to watch his son play. That night he went out to the garden and sat with Luca, Zinedine's son, two months old, asleep in his arms.

He writes that he could not take his eyes off the baby, and that his lips kept murmuring, without him meaning to: "Ah, your father, your father."

Here's what stays with me.

Forty-four years separate that half-built flat from that final. And on the biggest night of his son's life, the father spent it looking at a sleeping baby.
