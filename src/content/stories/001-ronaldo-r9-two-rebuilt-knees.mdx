On 12 April 2000, Ronaldo came off the bench in Rome. It was his first match since November.

Within minutes, his right knee buckled. The tendon, repaired five months earlier, had ruptured completely.

Two years after that night, he scored both goals in a World Cup final.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The boy who did all that was nearly lost to football over a bus fare.

Flamengo, the club he grew up supporting, let him go. His youth coach says he kept missing training because he could not afford the bus.

Cruzeiro took him instead, and in 1993 he scored five in one game. Then Europe: 30 league goals in his first PSV season, 47 in 49 games for Barcelona, and a world-record move to Inter in 1997.

And then his body started sending the bill.

In November 1999, against Lecce, his right knee gave way. Tendon ruptured. Five months later came Rome, and then 17 months without a competitive match.

The surgeon Gérard Saillant rebuilt the knee in Paris. His first league goal on it came in December 2001. On the last day of that season Inter lost the title, and Ronaldo wept on the bench.

A few weeks later he was at the World Cup.

Then came the part that surprised me.

He shaved his head, all except a patch at the front. Years later he explained why: once he turned up with it, nobody talked about his injury any more.

The haircut was a decoy.

In the final, Oliver Kahn, who had conceded one goal all tournament, failed to hold a shot from Rivaldo. Ronaldo scored from the rebound. Twelve minutes later he scored again. Brazil won 2–0.

Eight goals and the Golden Boot, on knees that had already failed him twice. When he retired in 2011, aged 34, he said he had lost the fight to his body.

Here's what stays with me. We usually measure a striker by what he did. Ronaldo makes us count what he was prevented from doing, and the sum never settles.

He was the best in the world before the knee went, and again after. Almost nobody manages that once.
