Ronaldo came off the bench in the second half of the first leg of the Coppa Italia final at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome on 12 April 2000. It was his first match since November. Within minutes, six by most accounts and seven by others, he shaped to turn and his right knee buckled beneath him. He went down clutching it. Lazio won the match 2–1, but the score hardly mattered: the patellar tendon in that knee, repaired after an injury against Lecce five months earlier, had ruptured completely.

He was still in his early twenties. Little more than three years earlier he had become the youngest player ever named FIFA World Player of the Year, and at 21 he had won the Ballon d'Or. He would not play another competitive match for 17 months.

## The phenomenon

Ronaldo Luís Nazário de Lima grew up in Rio de Janeiro supporting Flamengo, and Flamengo let him go. The story, as told by his youth coach at São Cristóvão, Alfredo Sampaio, is that the boy missed training at Flamengo because he could not afford the bus fare for the long journey, and the club offered no help. He stayed at São Cristóvão, the small club where he played youth football from 1990 to 1992, and in 1993 Cruzeiro of Belo Horizonte took him on. On 7 November that year, at the Mineirão, he scored five goals in a 6–0 win over Bahia. By the time he left, aged 17, he had 44 goals in 47 matches by the count most sources use; some Brazilian outlets give 46 matches, and higher totals appear when friendlies are included.

PSV Eindhoven bought him in 1994. He scored 30 league goals in his first Dutch season and finished as the Eredivisie's top scorer. In his second, a knee injury kept him out of much of the campaign. It was the first warning.

Barcelona signed him in 1996, and what followed was one of the great individual seasons of the modern game: 47 goals in 49 games in all competitions by the club's own count, 34 of them in the league. (ESPN's Spanish-language edition counts 48.) The goal everyone remembers came away at Compostela on 12 October 1996. Ronaldo picked the ball up deep, rode a trip and a tug from the first defender, ran past several more and scored. Barcelona won 5–1 and he added a second. On the touchline the coach, Bobby Robson, lifted his hands to his head in disbelief. At the end of the year FIFA named Ronaldo its World Player of the Year. He was 20, the youngest winner the award ever had. A year later he won it again, and the Ballon d'Or with it.

Inter paid a world-record fee, reported at £19.5 million, to take him to Milan in the summer of 1997. In his first season he scored 25 Serie A goals, two fewer than the league's top scorer, Oliver Bierhoff, and got the third goal as Inter beat Lazio 3–0 in the UEFA Cup final. Then came the World Cup in France.

## The final in France

The first team sheet Brazil handed in for the 1998 final against France did not include Ronaldo; Edmundo was listed to partner Bebeto. A revised sheet restored him before kick-off. What had happened in between has been argued over ever since, and only part of it is established. At the team hotel Ronaldo had suffered a convulsive episode. His room-mate, Roberto Carlos, raised the alarm, and Ronaldo was taken to hospital for tests before arriving at the stadium and saying he wanted to play. He played, and France won 3–0, Zinedine Zidane scoring twice with headers and Emmanuel Petit adding the third. Rumours about the cause of the episode have circulated for more than 25 years without being substantiated, and this story does not repeat them.

Sixteen months later came Lecce. On 21 November 1999, in a Serie A match, Ronaldo felt his right knee give and limped off. Tests showed a ruptured tendon, and he needed surgery. He came back for the cup final in April, and the knee lasted a matter of minutes.

## Seventeen months

The surgeon who repaired the knee after the second rupture was Gérard Saillant, in Paris. The recovery was slow and interrupted. Ronaldo did not play competitive football again until 20 September 2001, when he came on for the last 28 minutes of a UEFA Cup tie against Brașov, and his first return to league football that autumn was cut short by another, smaller injury after 13 minutes. On 9 December 2001 he started at Brescia and scored after 19 minutes of a 3–1 win. It was his first league goal since the afternoon against Lecce two years earlier.

By the spring he was scoring regularly, and four goals in three late-season matches kept Inter top of Serie A going into the final day. On 5 May 2002 they lost 4–2 at Lazio and Juventus took the title. Ronaldo was substituted in the second half and wept on the bench.

Brazil took him to the World Cup in South Korea and Japan a few weeks later.

He scored the equaliser in the opening 2–1 win over Turkey. He scored against China, twice against Costa Rica in a 5–2 win, and three minutes from the end of the 2–0 win over Belgium in the round of 16. In the semi-final against Turkey he scored the only goal, a toe-poke that caught the goalkeeper by surprise. He had been troubled by muscle injuries to his thigh in the months before the tournament, and during it he appeared with his head shaved except for a patch of hair at the front. Years later he said the haircut had been deliberate, a way of changing the subject from his leg: once he turned up to training with it, he said, nobody talked about the injury any more.

The final, against Germany on 30 June 2002, put him up against Oliver Kahn, who had conceded one goal in the tournament, an injury-time equaliser by Robbie Keane of Ireland in the group stage. In the 67th minute Kahn failed to hold a low shot from Rivaldo and Ronaldo scored from the rebound. In the 79th Kléberson's pass across the area was dummied by Rivaldo, and Ronaldo finished low. Brazil won 2–0. It was his eighth goal of the tournament and won him the Golden Boot, and FIFA named him World Player of the Year for the third time.

## The second career

Real Madrid signed him that summer. On 6 October 2002 he came on as a substitute against Alavés at the Bernabéu and scored twice in a 5–2 win, the first a minute after coming on. On 23 April 2003, in the second leg of a Champions League quarter-final, he scored a hat-trick at Old Trafford. Madrid lost 4–3 on the night but went through 6–5 on aggregate, and when he was substituted most of the home crowd rose to applaud him off. He was La Liga's top scorer in 2003–04 with 24 goals, and by the club's count he scored 104 goals in 177 games for Real Madrid.

At the 2006 World Cup, against Ghana, he scored his 15th World Cup goal, one more than Gerd Müller, a record that stood until Miroslav Klose scored his 16th against Brazil in 2014. On 13 February 2008, playing for Milan against Livorno at San Siro, he came on as a substitute and three minutes later ruptured the patellar tendon in his left knee. Eric Rolland operated on him in Paris, with Saillant assisting. Ronaldo went home to Brazil to play for Corinthians, and on 14 February 2011, aged 34, he announced his retirement, citing the injuries of the previous two years.

> Mentally, I wanted to continue, but I have to acknowledge that I lost [the fight] to my body.
> — Ronaldo, announcing his retirement, 14 February 2011, as reported by the Associated Press

## The turn

The turn in this story is not a moment of skill but a period of absence. When the tendon went for the second time in April 2000, Ronaldo had already won every individual award a player could win. What followed was surgery in Paris and 17 months away from competitive football, and even then the comeback stalled before it started. What he had not done was win a World Cup on the pitch, and less than seven months after his first league goal on the rebuilt knee he scored twice in a World Cup final. Everything after 2002, the hat-trick at Old Trafford, the record in Germany, the left knee giving way in Milan, happened on a body that had already failed him twice.

The what-if is unavoidable, and nobody can answer it. The numbers that exist are 62 goals in 98 games for Brazil by RSSSF's count, 15 at World Cups and three FIFA World Player of the Year awards, the last of them won with both goals in a World Cup final, two years after a knee injury that might have ended his career.

## What it tells us

Greatness in a striker is usually measured in what the player did. Ronaldo's case forces the other measurement, what the player was prevented from doing, and makes it impossible to settle. FIFA's vote named him the best player in the world at 20, and again after the knee had been rebuilt, which is a thing almost nobody manages once. The honest version of his story is not only the tragedy of the lost years in between, but what he did when they ended.
