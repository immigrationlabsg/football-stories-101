On 31 May 1992 the Yugoslavia squad were in Sweden, ten days from the start of the European Championship.

The United Nations had imposed sanctions the day before. UEFA confirmed the team was out.

Their place went to the runners-up in their qualifying group.

Denmark.

Legend says the Danes were called back from the beach.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

The beach part is mostly a myth.

As Peter Schmeichel put it to UEFA.com: “The myth is that we were all on the beach.”

The Danish league had finished only about two weeks before. The team already had a friendly arranged against the CIS, and drew it 1–1 on 3 June.

The coach, Richard Møller Nielsen, was not popular. Michael Laudrup, Denmark’s best player, refused to play under him.

The group stage went as expected. A 0–0 draw with England, a 1–0 defeat by Sweden.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Kim Vilfort had a young daughter, Line, who was being treated for leukaemia. After the second game her condition worsened, and he went home.

Without him, Denmark beat France 2–1 and reached the semi-finals.

His family urged him to go back.

The semi-final against the Netherlands, the holders, finished 2–2. Vilfort scored in the shoot-out. So did every other Dane.

Schmeichel saved from Marco van Basten, the only miss of the ten.

The final, in Gothenburg, was against Germany, the heavy favourites. John Jensen scored from long range after 18 minutes.

With 12 minutes left, Vilfort scored the second.

The father who had left for his daughter’s bedside had made it 2–0. Denmark, who had not qualified, were champions of Europe.

There was no fairy-tale ending for him. Line died a few weeks after the tournament.

Here's what stays with me.

The truer story is better than the legend. Not a beach, but ten days, a team unloved by its own star, and a father who kept leaving and coming back. Nobody expected anything of them, and that is its own kind of freedom.
