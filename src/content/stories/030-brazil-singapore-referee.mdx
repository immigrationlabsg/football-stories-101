On Tuesday 17 November, Brazil beat Singapore 7–0 at the National Stadium in Kallang.

Three days earlier, on the same pitch, a Singaporean referee had sent off their manager, Carlo Ancelotti.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

That Saturday, 14 November, Brazil played Japan in a friendly in front of 38,688 people. Takefusa Kubo and Keito Nakamura put Japan 2–0 up inside 19 minutes.

Vinícius Júnior scored twice. 2–2.

In stoppage time, Japan broke. Brazil said João Gomes was fouled in the build-up. The referee, Jansen Foo, played on, and the substitute Tatsuhiro Sakamoto scored his first goal for Japan. VAR let it stand. Japan won 3–2.

Brazil exploded. Foo sent off Ancelotti, and the captain Raphinha, who had already been substituted. Security staff had to step in.

Afterwards Ancelotti said it was not the referee's fault, then added: "It was a high-level match, and I don't think he had the experience to handle this kind of game."

Raphinha apologised for his behaviour, but said Foo made so many mistakes.

Then came the part that surprised me.

The man Ancelotti doubted is no newcomer. Foo has refereed since 2009, and the Football Association of Singapore lists him as one of its FIFA referees.

On Tuesday, Brazil were back at that stadium to face Singapore. Ancelotti was not on the bench; his assistant Paul Clement took charge. Raphinha was missing too.

Vinícius scored a hat-trick in 12 minutes. Endrick made it 4–0 before half-time. Estevão, Rayan and Allan added three more after the break.

Here's what stays with me.

On Saturday, Brazil blamed a Singaporean whistle. On Tuesday, Singapore paid.

Singapore's players had no part in Saturday's decisions. The 7–0 is theirs to carry anyway.
