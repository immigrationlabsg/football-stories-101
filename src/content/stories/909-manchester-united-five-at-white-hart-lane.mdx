Two goals down to Villa. Twice. And Manchester United still came back.

On Sunday night United drew 4–4 with Aston Villa at Old Trafford. Twice they went two goals down, and twice they came back. Patrick Dorgu levelled with a header from a corner in the 96th minute.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Opta says no team had ever done that in the Premier League: come from two goals behind to draw level twice in the same match.

Villa led 2–0 after four minutes. United were level by the 44th. Villa went 4–2 up after 53 minutes. Then Joshua Zirkzee scored in the second minute of added time, and Dorgu in the sixth. Both came off the bench. Even so, the draw leaves United 18th.

It sent me back to another United afternoon.

White Hart Lane, 29 September 2001. Spurs were brilliant in the first half.

Dean Richards scored after 15 minutes, on his Spurs debut. Les Ferdinand made it two after 25. Christian Ziege scored a third on the stroke of half-time.

Three nil. Over, surely.

Then came the part that still surprises me.

Straight after the break, Andy Cole headed one in. Laurent Blanc made it 3–2 just before the hour. Ruud van Nistelrooy made it 3–3 with 18 minutes left. Juan Sebastián Verón put United in front four minutes later.

And then David Beckham, three minutes from time. Five three.

Five goals in one half, away from home, from three down. Spurs had scored three in the first half and none in the second.

Here's what stays with me.

The two games are 25 years apart, and they are not the same kind of comeback. In 2001 United were three behind and won. On Sunday they were two behind, twice, and only drew. One ended in a win; the other ended in a record.

So which comeback was wilder? Spurs in 2001, or Villa on Sunday?
