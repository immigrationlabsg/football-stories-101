On 1 November 2003, Thierry Henry scored twice at Elland Road. Arsenal beat Leeds United 4–1 and stayed top of the league, unbeaten after 11 games.

On 1 November 2006, Kaká scored a hat-trick at San Siro. Milan beat Anderlecht 4–1 in the Champions League.

Same date. Three years apart. Five goals between them.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Each night came in a season that ended with its player on top.

Henry's first goal at Leeds came in the 8th minute. His second came before half-time, when Dennis Bergkamp's shot hit the post and fell to him. Robert Pires and Gilberto Silva got Arsenal's other goals.

For Leeds, it was a sixth defeat in seven games.

Leeds saw more of him that season. In April 2004, at Highbury, he scored four goals against them.

He finished with 30 league goals and the Golden Boot. Arsenal went the whole league season unbeaten.

Kaká's hat-trick, three years later, put Milan top of their group. Roland Juhász replied for Anderlecht, and Alberto Gilardino scored Milan's fourth.

By May, Milan had won the Champions League, beating Liverpool in the final in Athens.

Kaká finished as the competition's top scorer, with 10 goals.

Two forwards, two seasons, two teams on top.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Only one of them won the Ballon d'Or.

In 2003, Henry finished second, behind Pavel Nedvěd of Juventus.

He never won it, at Arsenal or anywhere else.

In 2007, Kaká won it.

And for the next ten years, every Ballon d'Or went to either Lionel Messi or Cristiano Ronaldo.

So Kaká was the last winner before the two of them took it in turns, and Henry stayed on the list of stars who never held it.

Here's what stays with me.

A vote decides one December.

It does not decide who was better to watch.

That argument stays open, and it belongs to the fans.
