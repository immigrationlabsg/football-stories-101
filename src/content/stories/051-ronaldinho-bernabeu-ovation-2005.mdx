On 19 November 2005, the Santiago Bernabéu rose to applaud a Barcelona player.

Real Madrid's own fans. In their own stadium. While their team was losing 3–0.

The player was Ronaldinho. He had just scored his second goal, going past Sergio Ramos for the second time.

Samuel Eto'o had scored the other.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

He was the first Barcelona player to be applauded there since Diego Maradona.

In June 1983, in a League Cup final, Maradona rounded Real Madrid's goalkeeper. A defender, Juan José, slid in to stop him and crashed into the post. Maradona scored, and the Bernabéu clapped. Barcelona went on to win the cup.

It took 22 years to happen again.

That night in 2005, Vanderlei Luxemburgo's Real Madrid fielded Zinedine Zidane, Ronaldo, Raúl, Roberto Carlos and David Beckham.

Frank Rijkaard's Barcelona went ahead early. An 18-year-old Lionel Messi, in his first Clásico, set up Eto'o.

Just before the hour, Ronaldinho picked up the ball on the left, ran at Ramos, went past him and Iván Helguera, and beat Iker Casillas.

With 13 minutes left he did it again. In from the left, past Ramos, into the far corner.

Barcelona led 3–0. That was when many of the home fans rose.

But here's where it gets interesting.

Two years earlier, the signing Barcelona's fans had been promised was Beckham.

In 2003 Joan Laporta ran for president of Barcelona promising to sign Beckham. Manchester United accepted his offer, if he won and agreed terms with the player.

Laporta won. Beckham signed for Real Madrid instead.

Barcelona signed Ronaldinho from Paris Saint-Germain that summer.

So at the Bernabéu that night, the player Laporta had promised was in Madrid's starting line-up.

The player they got instead was the one Madrid's fans stood up for.

Here's what stays with me.

You cannot buy applause from your rivals.

And for a few minutes, the Bernabéu forgot whose side it was on.
