On 19 November 1969, Pelé scored from the penalty spot for Santos against Vasco da Gama at the Maracanã.

It was the 1,000th goal of his career. It also won the game: Santos beat Vasco 2–1, in front of more than 65,000 people.

On 19 November 2013, Cristiano Ronaldo scored a hat-trick in Solna, just outside Stockholm. Portugal beat Zlatan Ibrahimović's Sweden 3–2 and went to the World Cup.

Same date. One goal, then three. 44 years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Pelé's thousandth goal was not a dribble or a volley. It came from the plainest chance in football.

Ronaldo's night was a duel. It was the second leg of a play-off, and he had scored the only goal of the first leg, in Lisbon.

In Solna he scored five minutes into the second half. Then Ibrahimović scored twice, and Sweden led 2–1 on the night.

Ronaldo answered with two more goals. Portugal went through 4–2 on aggregate.

The hat-trick took him level with Pauleta's Portugal record of 47 goals.

Then came the part that surprised me.

That hat-trick was never meant to count for the Ballon d'Or.

Voting for the 2013 award was due to close on 15 November, four days before Solna. Then FIFA moved the deadline to 29 November. The hat-trick could now be taken into account.

On 13 January 2014, in Zurich, Ronaldo won the Ballon d'Or for the second time. Lionel Messi had won the four before.

The same night, at the same ceremony, Pelé was given an honorary award, the Prix d'Honneur.

He had never been eligible for the real thing. Until 1995, the Ballon d'Or was open only to Europeans.

Here's what stays with me.

Pelé needed one kick to reach a thousand.

Ronaldo's three goals came after the vote was meant to be over, and they still counted.

They were honoured on the same night, 44 years after that penalty at the Maracanã.
