On 25 November 2005, George Best died in a London hospital. He was 59.

On 25 November 2020, Diego Maradona died at his home on the outskirts of Buenos Aires. He was 60. Argentina declared three days of national mourning.

Same date. Fifteen years apart.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

Each man won a prize the other never did.

Best was 22 when Manchester United won the 1968 European Cup. He scored in extra time of the final, a 4–1 win over Benfica at Wembley.

That year he also won the Ballon d'Or.

But he never played at a World Cup. He won 37 caps for Northern Ireland and scored 9 goals.

Maradona played at four World Cups, from 1982 to 1994. In 1986, as Argentina's captain, he won it in Mexico.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Maradona never won the Ballon d'Or.

Until 1995, only European players could. That year France Football gave him an honorary one instead.

Much later, the magazine looked back and concluded he would have won it twice.

He never won the European Cup either. His only European trophy was the UEFA Cup, with Napoli in 1989. He also led Napoli to their first Serie A title in 1987, and a second in 1990.

So Best had the European Cup and the Ballon d'Or, but no World Cup. Maradona had the World Cup, but neither of the others.

Two cities found their own way to remember them.

In 2006 Belfast, where Best was born, renamed its city airport George Best Belfast City Airport.

Italian rules normally make a city wait ten years after a death before naming a place after someone. Naples made an exception. In December 2020 its stadium became the Stadio Diego Armando Maradona.

Here's what stays with me.

Even talent like theirs leaves a gap.

Each man holds the prize the other is missing.

And now they share a date as well.
