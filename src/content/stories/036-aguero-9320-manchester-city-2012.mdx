At 90:00 on 13 May 2012, Manchester City were losing 2–1 at home to Queens Park Rangers.

QPR had been down to ten men for more than half an hour.

At Sunderland, Manchester United had already won. If nothing changed, the title was United's.

And the away manager had a history with City.

Here's what I didn't know until recently.

He was Mark Hughes. City had sacked him in December 2009 to appoint Roberto Mancini.

In April City were eight points behind United with six to play. Then United lost at Wigan and drew 4–4 with Everton. Vincent Kompany's header won the derby.

By the last day the clubs were level on points. City were at home to a side fighting relegation.

Pablo Zabaleta put City ahead. Djibril Cissé equalised after half-time.

Then QPR's Joey Barton was sent off for an elbow on Carlos Tevez.

Ten men, away at the leaders. It should have been over.

Then came the part that surprised me.

Jamie Mackie headed QPR in front.

Mancini sent on Edin Džeko and Mario Balotelli. QPR held on.

Two minutes into stoppage time, Džeko headed in a corner. A draw was no use.

Sergio Agüero found Balotelli, who pushed the ball back as he fell. It was Balotelli's only Premier League assist.

Agüero took one touch past Taye Taiwo and scored. The clock read 93:20.

Both clubs finished on 89 points. City won on goal difference, the first Premier League title decided that way. Their first league title in 44 years.

The last, in 1968, had also come on the final day, with a 4–3 win at Newcastle.

In between, City fell into the third tier. In 1999 they were two down in a Wembley play-off final with the 90 minutes almost gone. Kevin Horlock scored, then Paul Dickov. City won on penalties.

The club's lowest point and its highest, both rescued after the 90 minutes were up.

Here's what stays with me. A season is 38 matches long, and this one is remembered as 200 seconds. Sometimes the bravest thing a team can do is keep playing for the goal it needs.
