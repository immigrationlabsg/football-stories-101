# Growth log

What we changed to grow the page, why, and what the numbers did afterwards. The weekly review (Mondays) adds a section
here: the **Audience report** figures for the week, what worked, what didn't, and the next experiment. One change at a
time where possible, so each result can be traced to its cause.

## The owner's brief (27 Oct 2026)

"Be the greatest influencer in the world: grow the popularity and reach of this account, and bring great football
history, stories and comparisons to more football fanatics and readers in the world. Keep wowing us, keep our attention,
and bring us these amazing stories every day." Growth is our job, not the owner's: no asking friends to follow, no
engagement bait; reach comes from stories worth sending to a friend, strong first seconds, and learning from the
numbers every week. The owner does not want to be reminded about the Facebook password / exposed keys (decided 27 Oct).

## Standing instruction (owner, 30 Oct 2026)

"Always review on your own work and the results and research on other successful examples, and work on improving the
results and testing on your own." So every week, without being asked: read our numbers, study at least one page that
does better, pick one change, test it, and log what happened here.

## How every decision is made: the four asks (owner, 28 Oct 2026)

Before any growth decision, answer: (1) what a top professional (head of social at a big football account) would do
here that we are not doing; (2) the rawest material to start from, not what is already digested; (3) the specific
methods and tools they would use; (4) what, other than us, would show the result is right. Then act on that.

Standing brief that came out of it: grow the page fans send to their mates. Each week start from raw material
(per-Reel retention curves, fan arguments on big football accounts, this week's fixtures and news, primary sources),
change ONE thing (hook, Reel length, topical tie-in, the caption's first line, posting time), test it with Instagram
Trial Reels where possible, and judge it only by strangers' behaviour: shares and sends per 1,000 reached, average watch
time as a share of Reel length, saves, follows per post, and growth against comparable football-history pages.

Experiments queued (one per week, in this order):
1. Topical tie-in: the weekly drafting run checks the coming week's big fixtures and news and gives at least one story a
   link to them ("Arsenal play Liverpool on Saturday: the night in 1989..."). Within the format; no OK needed.
2. Caption first line names the player and the moment (Instagram search). Within the format.
3. ~~Shorter Reel~~ Owner, 28 Oct 2026: keep the full Reel (about 25 s: cover 3 s, cards 4 s each), but put the weight
   on the first 4-6 seconds (cover + fact card 1) and the first 20 seconds (facts 1-4). From the 31 Oct drafting run,
   fact card 1 is the most surprising fact in under ~20 words (docs/drafting-brief.md). *Watch:* Facebook average watch
   time (2-6 s in week 1) and Instagram views per reach, new drafts against older ones.
4. Posting time, once Instagram shows where followers live. Bring to the owner with evidence (owner, 28 Oct 2026:
   proposals come with evidence; keep refining experiments without asking).

## Research, 30 Oct 2026 (web search; pages could not be opened, so figures are from search summaries)

Evidence marked [P] primary (Meta, Mosseri, peer-reviewed), [D] large vendor dataset, [S] secondary.
- Ranking: watch time, likes and sends are Instagram's top signals; sends per reach matter most for reaching
  non-followers (Mosseri, Jan 2025) [P]. Instagram's skip rate counts viewers who leave in the first 3 s [S].
- Captions: at most five hashtags (Instagram cap, Dec 2025) and no links; hashtags add little reach (Mosseri) [P/S].
  Ours: up to five, no links. Reposted or agency-style content stops being recommended (2024-2026) [P/S]; our own
  paintings are safe.
- Carousels: 6.9% engagement per person reached against 3.3% for Reels (Buffer, 52M posts, 2026), while Reels get
  more reach [D].
- Threads: Meta says replying is "just as important as posting"; topic tags help [P].
- Nostalgia anchored to a date gets sent ("20 Years Ago Sports", ~700 to ~400k in two weeks) [S]; a numbered daily ritual
  brings people back (The United Strand) [S]. Asking fans to comment or take a quiz did not work in a study of 64,778
  NFL Instagram posts [P], so quizzes must pay off inside the Reel (ours reveal at the end).
- Not available yet: Trial Reels (need 1,000 followers).

**Ideas, ranked by likely effect on shares and follows**

1. **Pick stories for a high-energy feeling: awe, anger or tension. Avoid stories that only leave people sad.**
   - Others: the biggest videos on the pages below are awe (Iniesta 2010, Messi), outrage (the "Battle of Nuremberg", the "Red Card King") or shock. "The Dirtiest Match in World Cup History" earned 274.1K views; the same account's usual floor is about 3,000-8,000.
   - Source: Berger and Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?", Journal of Marketing Research, 2012. They studied all New York Times articles over three months. "Content that evokes high-arousal positive (awe) or negative (anger or anxiety) emotions is more viral." Sadness is "less viral." https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf
   - Strength: **study**, but on emailed news articles, not Reels. One counterpoint: Diez Ball's top video (Iniesta, 3.8M) is a tribute to a friend who died, so grief mixed with triumph also travels.
   - Test: the drafting run labels each story's main feeling. For one week, every story is awe or outrage. Compare shares per 1,000 reached with the trial week.

2. **A countdown series tied to a dated event ("Day 3/10").**
   - Others: Diez Ball (TikTok, 216.7K followers) ran "20 stories in 20 days before the world cup". Episodes had 35.6K-543.6K views up to Day 11. From Day 12 they jumped: 1.1M, 1.4M and 522.3K for Days 12-14, then 441.5K-3.8M for Days 15-20. Day 18 was not among the 20 of 22 episodes that loaded.
   - Source: https://www.tiktok.com/@diez.ball (playlist "World Cup Stories", 22 videos).
   - Strength: **one page**. The rise can't be separated from the World Cup getting closer, and the page has a presenter on camera, which we don't.
   - Test: pick a dated event after 17 Nov from the fixtures (a derby, a final, a draw). Run 7-10 numbered stories that end on it, with "Day n/10" on the first frame and in the caption. Compare follows over those days with the trial. Never ask for a follow.

3. **If the skip rate doesn't fall, test the first 3 seconds alone.**
   - Others: Instagram's own advice is to "Start with a compelling hook in the first three seconds", to make Reels that stand on their own for people who don't follow you, and to watch the view rate after 3 seconds. The Professional Dashboard tips (reported July 2024) begin "Grab attention in the first 3 seconds". On football TikTok, the hook is often a true superlative ("The most dangerous defender in the world: the Red Card King", 9M views on @legoat2215).
   - Sources: Instagram for Creators, 26 Feb 2025, https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through; Social Media Today, 10 Jul 2024, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-tips-for-reels-creators/721054/; https://www.tiktok.com/@legoat2215.
   - Strength: **platform** for the 3-second rule; **one page** for superlatives. That page mostly uses match footage, which we never use.
   - Test: keep the body of the Reel. Rotate the type of line 0 (a true superlative, the result first, a question) across a week. Compare skip rate and average watch time. Every superlative needs a fact-check entry; drop it if a source doesn't say "most" or "only".
   - Already in our favour: a vendor study of 10,110 Facebook Reels from 704 brand Pages (Emplifi, reported 22 Mar 2026) found human speech in the first three seconds got a "better response", and speech beat music on 10-second retention by almost 25%.
