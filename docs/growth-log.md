# Growth log

What we changed to grow the page, why, and what the numbers did afterwards. The weekly review (Mondays) adds a section
here: the **Audience report** figures for the week, what worked, what didn't, and the next experiment. One change at a
time where possible, so each result can be traced to its cause.

## The owner's brief (27 Oct 2026)

"Be the greatest influencer in the world: grow the popularity and reach of this account, and bring great football
history, stories and comparisons to more football fanatics and readers in the world. Keep wowing us, keep our attention,
and bring us these amazing stories every day." Growth is our job, not the owner's: no asking friends to follow, no
engagement bait; reach comes from stories worth sending to a friend, strong first seconds, and learning from the
numbers every week. The owner does not want to be reminded about the Facebook password / exposed keys (decided 27 Oct).

## Standing instruction (owner, 30 Oct 2026)

"Always review on your own work and the results and research on other successful examples, and work on improving the
results and testing on your own." So every week, without being asked: read our numbers, study at least one page that
does better, pick one change, test it, and log what happened here.

## How every decision is made: the four asks (owner, 28 Oct 2026)

Before any growth decision, answer: (1) what a top professional (head of social at a big football account) would do
here that we are not doing; (2) the rawest material to start from, not what is already digested; (3) the specific
methods and tools they would use; (4) what, other than us, would show the result is right. Then act on that.

Standing brief that came out of it: grow the page fans send to their mates. Each week start from raw material
(per-Reel retention curves, fan arguments on big football accounts, this week's fixtures and news, primary sources),
change ONE thing (hook, Reel length, topical tie-in, the caption's first line, posting time), test it with Instagram
Trial Reels where possible, and judge it only by strangers' behaviour: shares and sends per 1,000 reached, average watch
time as a share of Reel length, saves, follows per post, and growth against comparable football-history pages.

Experiments queued (one per week, in this order):
1. Topical tie-in: the weekly drafting run checks the coming week's big fixtures and news and gives at least one story a
   link to them ("Arsenal play Liverpool on Saturday: the night in 1989..."). Within the format; no OK needed.
2. Caption first line names the player and the moment (Instagram search). Within the format.
3. ~~Shorter Reel~~ Owner, 28 Oct 2026: keep the full Reel (about 25 s: cover 3 s, cards 4 s each), but put the weight
   on the first 4-6 seconds (cover + fact card 1) and the first 20 seconds (facts 1-4). From the 31 Oct drafting run,
   fact card 1 is the most surprising fact in under ~20 words (docs/drafting-brief.md). *Watch:* Facebook average watch
   time (2-6 s in week 1) and Instagram views per reach, new drafts against older ones.
4. Posting time, once Instagram shows where followers live. Bring to the owner with evidence (owner, 28 Oct 2026:
   proposals come with evidence; keep refining experiments without asking).

## Research, 30 Oct 2026 (web search; pages could not be opened, so figures are from search summaries)

Evidence marked [P] primary (Meta, Mosseri, peer-reviewed), [D] large vendor dataset, [S] secondary.
- Ranking: watch time, likes and sends are Instagram's top signals; sends per reach matter most for reaching
  non-followers (Mosseri, Jan 2025) [P]. Instagram's skip rate counts viewers who leave in the first 3 s [S].
- Captions: at most five hashtags (Instagram cap, Dec 2025) and no links; hashtags add little reach (Mosseri) [P/S].
  Ours: up to five, no links. Reposted or agency-style content stops being recommended (2024-2026) [P/S]; our own
  paintings are safe.
- Carousels: 6.9% engagement per person reached against 3.3% for Reels (Buffer, 52M posts, 2026), while Reels get
  more reach [D].
- Threads: Meta says replying is "just as important as posting"; topic tags help [P].
- Nostalgia anchored to a date gets sent ("20 Years Ago Sports", ~700 to ~400k in two weeks) [S]; a numbered daily ritual
  brings people back (The United Strand) [S]. Asking fans to comment or take a quiz did not work in a study of 64,778
  NFL Instagram posts [P], so quizzes must pay off inside the Reel (ours reveal at the end).
- Not available yet: Trial Reels (need 1,000 followers).

**Ideas, ranked by likely effect on shares and follows**

1. **Pick stories for a high-energy feeling: awe, anger or tension. Avoid stories that only leave people sad.**
   - Others: the biggest videos on the pages below are awe (Iniesta 2010, Messi), outrage (the "Battle of Nuremberg", the "Red Card King") or shock. "The Dirtiest Match in World Cup History" earned 274.1K views; the same account's usual floor is about 3,000-8,000.
   - Source: Berger and Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?", Journal of Marketing Research, 2012. They studied all New York Times articles over three months. "Content that evokes high-arousal positive (awe) or negative (anger or anxiety) emotions is more viral." Sadness is "less viral." https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf
   - Strength: **study**, but on emailed news articles, not Reels. One counterpoint: Diez Ball's top video (Iniesta, 3.8M) is a tribute to a friend who died, so grief mixed with triumph also travels.
   - Test: the drafting run labels each story's main feeling. For one week, every story is awe or outrage. Compare shares per 1,000 reached with the trial week.

2. **A countdown series tied to a dated event ("Day 3/10").**
   - Others: Diez Ball (TikTok, 216.7K followers) ran "20 stories in 20 days before the world cup". Episodes had 35.6K-543.6K views up to Day 11. From Day 12 they jumped: 1.1M, 1.4M and 522.3K for Days 12-14, then 441.5K-3.8M for Days 15-20. Day 18 was not among the 20 of 22 episodes that loaded.
   - Source: https://www.tiktok.com/@diez.ball (playlist "World Cup Stories", 22 videos).
   - Strength: **one page**. The rise can't be separated from the World Cup getting closer, and the page has a presenter on camera, which we don't.
   - Test: pick a dated event after 17 Nov from the fixtures (a derby, a final, a draw). Run 7-10 numbered stories that end on it, with "Day n/10" on the first frame and in the caption. Compare follows over those days with the trial. Never ask for a follow.

3. **If the skip rate doesn't fall, test the first 3 seconds alone.**

The Professional Dashboard tips (reported July 2024) begin "Grab attention in the first 3 seconds". On football TikTok, the hook is often a true superlative ("The most dangerous defender in the world: the Red Card King", 9M views on @legoat2215).
   - Sources: Instagram for Creators, 26 Feb 2025, https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through; Social Media Today, 10 Jul 2024, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-tips-for-reels-creators/721054/; https://www.tiktok.com/@legoat2215.
   - Strength: **platform** for the 3-second rule; **one page** for superlatives. That page mostly uses match footage, which we never use.
   - Test: keep the body of the Reel. Rotate the type of line 0 (a true superlative, the result first, a question) across a week. Compare skip rate and average watch time. Every superlative needs a fact-check entry; drop it if a source doesn't say "most" or "only".
   - Already in our favour: a vendor study of 10,110 Facebook Reels from 704 brand Pages (Emplifi, reported 22 Mar 2026) found human speech in the first three seconds got a "better response", and speech beat music on 10-second retention by almost 25%. ## Mosseri interview notes, 9 Nov 2026 (owner: "take what he said as serious context")

Source: Colin and Samir's interview with Adam Mosseri, 26 Oct 2026 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxizdQ2hRfk, 71:39; also https://www.instagram.com/p/De8mKLVCW8X/). Read from YouTube's auto-captions on 9 Nov, so wording is close, not word-perfect. Minutes are approximate. The decisions drawn from it are in `docs/improvement-loop.md` section 10.
- About 22:50, carousels: with music they are "eligible to show up" in the Reels viewer. They get a "second chance" (a second position if you scroll past). Their depth means they are "likely to inspire you to send it to a friend".
- About 25 min, focused accounts: "be really really clear about who you want to be, what your goal is", then pick the tools that stay true to that.
- About 28 min: far more photos and videos are sent in DMs than posted to stories or Reels.
- About 34-35 min, ranking: "People think we rank just to drive engagement, but we actually rank for a lot of other things. Originality is a really key one." Original creators get "more distribution than aggregators". "Breakouts is another key one": every piece of content gets a chance, and "a brand new account can get millions of views". "Recency is another one. If you want to be culturally relevant, you need the ecosystem to ebb and flow with what's happening in the world right now."
- About 37 min: Instagram checks "the percentage of followers" you reach, so "having a follower count on Instagram means something even in a recommendations-first world".
- About 40 min: "You don't have to use all of the formats." Judge every tool against your creative identity and your business.
- About 42 min, watch time: Instagram focuses on it less than its competitors, "but we do care about watch time". It looks at "how likely you are to watch most of the video", and also cares "about sends and comments and likes".
- About 44 min, what makes a great Instagram show (Subway Takes): people can engage immediately; "it reminds you of a friend" and "you immediately want to send it".
- About 46 min: to reach people who don't follow you, "sends per reach is ... unconnected reach". Edits "now do the math for you", and it is coming to Instagram Insights. For a new creator the metrics to follow are "sends per reach, likes per reach and then ... watch time". Returning viewers count indirectly: viewing history predicts what you'll watch.
- About 53-56 min, AI: "we don't rank them differently". Instagram is "starting to require AI accounts or AI generated characters to label themselves". New kinds of AI content are hard to rank, and it uses like rates and "worth your time" survey scores. "Originality is probably the principal answer" to stolen material.
- About 69-70 min: "what works today might not work in 6 months". He reinvented his creator spotlight from Story to green-screen video to carousel when "what resonates with people changed".
- Mosseri, 6 Nov 2026 post: Instagram has "cleaned up Insights for creators and businesses".
- Instagram Original Content Guidelines (https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines): narration, a storyline or creative graphics that re-contextualise count as original. Borders, watermarks, speed changes or only crediting do not. Unoriginal accounts lose recommendations to non-followers until a "significant percentage" of the last 30 days is original. Check Account Status.

### Recommendation

The trial **did not hurt anything and clearly lifted watching** (skip down about 9 points, watch time about doubled),
but likes per post (median) and shares did not rise across the board, and followers are still in single figures. So:
**keep the voiced Zidane-style format, and start testing what makes a post break out**, one change at a time, measured
by shares and likes per first 120 views, with skip / stayed-to-watch as the early sign.

### How we revise

**a. Why people share football posts** (evidence label in brackets):
- High-arousal feelings (awe, anger, surprise) are shared more than low-arousal ones such as sadness [study: Berger and
  Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?", JMR 2012, https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf].
- Fans share to show identity and support their team, and to connect with other fans [study: Clavio and Frederick 2014,
  college football check-ins; Kim, Cho and Kim 2021 on sports hashtags, summarised at
  https://trace.tennessee.edu/jasm/vol6/iss2/18].
- Instagram's top three signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach; sends weigh slightly more than likes
  for people who don't follow you, but watch time matters most for everyone [platform: Mosseri, Jan 2025, reported at
  https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/].
- A myth fans already argue about, settled with a fact (AGH Stories' Pelé Short, about 1.25M views, 3.8 times its
  median) and paradox titles travel [one page, Benchmark 16 Nov above].
- Our own: the one share-earner (021) was an underdog story a fan could send to "anyone who says nobody expected them".

**b. What our share-earners and breakouts had.** Instagram: 021 (25 shares) and 908/028 (reach without shares) were all
world-known moments told from an angle most fans don't know, with skip of 40% or less. TikTok's only share (028) is the
same Messi Reel. YouTube: the same three plus 023 and 025 had the best "stayed to watch". Facebook: nothing earned a
share; posting method decided plays.

**c. Ranked changes** (one at a time, each measured over 7 daily posts by shares and likes per first 120 views, with
Instagram skip and YouTube stayed-to-watch as early signs; the bar stays at least 3 shares from at least 2 posts):
1. **Story choice: a world-known moment or name + a lesser-known fact about it (the Zidane model), every day.** Why: all
   three breakouts had it; the weakest posts didn't. Evidence: moderate (3 posts, 2 networks agree; AGH Stories points the
   same way). Effort: low, already the rule since 16 Nov. Test: test 1 (23-29 Nov, story pick by feeling) as registered;
   compare against 029-034.
2. **Facebook: post on the day (directly, or scheduled 7 h or less ahead) instead of a day or more ahead.** Why: 7 of 8
   such posts got 190-276 plays, against 0 of 7 posts scheduled further ahead getting more than 6. Evidence: moderate
   (15 posts, but confounded with evening extras). Effort: low. Test: one week of Facebook-only change; needs the owner's
   OK because Facebook is the control network.
3. **Ending question that makes the viewer take a side** ("Would you still have given him the Golden Ball?", "Cheat or
   genius?") instead of "name your own example". Why: a side-taking question gives a reason to send it to someone who
   disagrees. Evidence: weak (only a lead from AGH Stories and Berger and Milkman; our 908 got 0 shares with this question).
   Effort: low. Test: after test 1.
4. **Famous name in the first line on screen.** Why: 2 of 3 top posts. Evidence: weak to moderate. Effort: low.
5. **Drop the quiz format and two-date pairs without a surprise.** Evidence: moderate (quiz highest skip twice, 022 and 026).
