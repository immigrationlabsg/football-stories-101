## Suspected problems found on an editorial read (each still needs sources)

- Wembley 1953: the drag-back goal past Billy Wright is usually given as Puskás's **first**
  goal, not "the second of them". The ball went inside the near post, which also needs checking against "into the roof of the net".
- "Fire engine going to the wrong fire": check the exact wording, that it described Wright
  and that it appeared in Geoffrey Green's report in The Times of 26 November 1953 (Times reports were unsigned then).
- Bobby Robson quote: check the exact wording and where it was first recorded. As it stands it has no date or outlet.
- "First side from outside the British Isles to win there": check the wording (Ireland won at Goodison in 1949).
- 1960 final: the order of Puskás's four goals is probably wrong (a shot before half-time, then a penalty, a header, then a fourth). Check attendance 127,621 and Eintracht's 12 goals against Rangers.
- 1954 final: the disallowed equaliser is sometimes put at the 86th or 87th minute rather than the 88th. Check Liebrich and the 8–3, Griffiths as linesman, and 2–0 after eight minutes.
- FIFA/Hungarian ban: who imposed it, for how long, and when it ended. Also check "Inter Milan looked, and looked away".
- Spelling and role of Emil Österreicher (Östreicher) at Real Madrid in 1958.
- League goals in 1958–59 (21) and 1959–60 (26), the four Pichichi awards, five titles 1961–65, and 156 in 180.
- 1966: whether he was still in the squad, and the claim that his "name was on the roll of honour for a third time".
- Only player with hat-tricks in two European Cup finals; four Spain caps with three at the 1962 World Cup; 84 in 85.
- 1981 visit "first time in 25 years", return "early 1990s", manager in 1993, stadium renamed 2002, death 17 November 2006 aged 79, burial in St Stephen's Basilica.
- "Kocsis and Czibor ... good rather than great" is an unsupported judgement; soften or cut.
- "The decision was made in Brussels and Milan": check where the squad was.
- Sources list: none of the listed web pages were opened; the Times report and the books were not consulted.
