# Fact-check log: 015 Boca Juniors / The colours from a Swedish ship, and how much of the story is true

Checked 18 October 2026. Method note: WebFetch and curl could not open any page from this session (network policy blocks the hosts), and BBC, Guardian, Reuters, Telegraph and Independent are excluded from the search tool; clarin.com, ole.com.ar and nytimes.com were also refused by the search tool. Every claim was checked against the text of pages **as returned in web-search result excerpts**, not against the full pages. Wikipedia and Kiddle appeared in results and were used only as pointers. About 18 searches were used on this story.

## Documented versus legend (as stated in the story)

- **Documented:** founding on 3 April 1905 by sons of (mostly Genoese) Italian immigrants; early white-with-black-stripes shirts and other designs; adoption of blue and gold in 1907 on Brichetto's proposal (centenary book: 5 April 1907); diagonal sash first, horizontal band from 1912/13.
- **Legend / unconfirmed:** the match against Nottingham de Almagro (or Racing) that Boca lost; the "next ship into port" rule; the ship's name (Drottning Sophia or Princessan Ingeborg) and date (23 March 1907). Mediotiempo reports that the ship story gains ground only from 1934 and appears in no 1907 newspaper, club minutes, book or magazine. Izquierda Diario notes historians' records of Swedish ships entering Buenos Aires in early 1907 (plausibility, not proof). The story says all this explicitly.
