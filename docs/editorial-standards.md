# Editorial standards

The short version of sections 5.1 to 5.5 of the build specification, kept here
so it lives next to the stories.

## The story template

1. **Headline** in the "Subject / angle" form: `Ferenc Puskás / The exile who scored 156 goals for Real Madrid`.
2. **Dek**: one sentence, 160 characters or fewer. The counter-intuitive hook or the striking number.
3. **Opening**: start inside a specific moment, with a date and a place. No throat-clearing.
4. **Body**: 1,200 to 1,800 words, chronological or hinge-and-flashback, with two or three pull quotes from sourced interviews or contemporary reports.
5. **The turn**: the decision, accident or trait that explains the outcome. A heading `## The turn` is the convention.
6. **Close**: a heading `## What it tells us`, two to four sentences in the present tense, then the one-line question to the reader (the `question` field).
7. **Sources**: every story lists its sources. Two independent sources for every factual claim; disagreements stated in the text.
8. **Illustration**: one hero image, square-friendly, used for the web, the email, Instagram and the share card.

## Style

- Third person. Past tense for history, present tense for "What it tells us".
- British English spelling.
- Numerals for scores, ages, fees and attendances: 7–3, aged 31, £1 million, 127,621.
- Full names on first mention, surnames after.
- No "I". No exclamation marks.
- Quotations short, verbatim and attributed to where they were first recorded.

## The living-persons rule (non-negotiable)

Any allegation about a living person (match-fixing, doping, misconduct, private
life) is included only if all three hold:

1. it is an established fact from a reputable primary source: a conviction, an official finding or the person's own admission;
2. it is phrased as attributed fact ("was banned by UEFA in 2011 after…"), never as insinuation;
3. the story needs it.

Otherwise leave it out. Prefer documented events and, where in doubt, deceased
subjects. Singapore treats republication as publication, and defamation awards
against individual bloggers have reached six figures.

## Images

- Default hero: the publication's own commissioned or generated illustration.
- Photographs only from Wikimedia Commons (credit author, licence and source link), from the owner's own camera, or under a paid licence. No Getty editorial of English football without the extra FAPL licence. No match footage anywhere.
- No club crests, as design elements or in share images. Use club names, colour swatches or kit silhouettes.
- Alt text is mandatory and must describe the picture.

## AI assistance

AI tools may help with structure and prose, never with facts. Every fact in a
published story must trace to a source in the research brief. The About page
says so publicly.
