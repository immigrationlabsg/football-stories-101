# Editorial standards

The short version of sections 5.1 to 5.5 of the build specification, kept here
so it lives next to the stories.

## The story template

The model is The Morning Founder: a fast story told by a friend who has just found out something remarkable.

1. **Headline** in the "Subject / angle" form: `Ferenc Puskás / The exile who scored 156 goals for Real Madrid`.
2. **Dek**: one sentence, 160 characters or fewer. The counter-intuitive hook or the striking number.
3. **Hook (first 50 to 100 words):** two to five very short paragraphs that set a scene or a striking fact and
   make the reader ask "who?" or "how?".
4. **The reveal line, early:** straight after the hook, about 15% of the way in, a line of its own:
   "Here's what I didn't know until recently." (variants: "Here's what I only found out recently.", "Here's the
   part nobody told me."). Then the backstory that answers the hook.
5. **Body:** 500 to 700 words in total. No section headings. Paragraphs of one to three short sentences; single-line
   paragraphs for punch ("He stayed 35 years."). Around the middle, a second turn on a line of its own: "Then came the
   part that surprised me." or "But here's where it gets interesting." At most one quote, woven in.
6. **Close**: "Here's what stays with me." then two to four short sentences of reflection, present tense. Then the
   one-line question to the reader (the `question` field, not typed into the body).
7. **Sources**: every story lists its sources in the `sources` field; they appear at the foot of the web page, not in
   the email or social posts. Two independent sources for every factual claim.
8. **Picture**: one hero image used for the web, the email, Instagram and the share card.

## Style

- **Voice:** first person, as "the Editor". The narrator reacts and reflects ("Here's what I didn't know", "Here's
  what stays with me", "Then came the part that surprised me"). The narrator **never invents personal experiences**:
  no "when I was a kid", no "I was there", no memories, travels or opinions attributed to a real person. The owner
  stays out of frame; the "I" is the publication's editor.
- Past tense for history, present tense for the reflection.
- British English spelling.
- Numerals for scores, ages, fees and attendances: 7–3, aged 31, £1 million, 127,621.
- Full names on first mention, surnames after.
- No exclamation marks. No clichés ("the rest is history", "etched in folklore").
- Quotations short, verbatim and attributed in the sentence ("As Puskás put it: …").

## The living-persons rule (non-negotiable)

Any allegation about a living person (match-fixing, doping, misconduct, private
life) is included only if all three hold:

1. it is an established fact from a reputable primary source: a conviction, an official finding or the person's own admission;
2. it is phrased as attributed fact ("was banned by UEFA in 2011 after…"), never as insinuation;
3. the story needs it.

Otherwise leave it out. Prefer documented events and, where in doubt, deceased
subjects. Singapore treats republication as publication, and defamation awards
against individual bloggers have reached six figures.

## Images

- Default hero: the publication's own commissioned or generated illustration.
- Photographs only from Wikimedia Commons (credit author, licence and source link), from the owner's own camera, or under a paid licence. No Getty editorial of English football without the extra FAPL licence. No match footage anywhere.
- No club crests, as design elements or in share images. Use club names, colour swatches or kit silhouettes.
- Alt text is mandatory and must describe the picture.

## AI assistance

AI tools may help with structure and prose, never with facts. Every fact in a
published story must trace to a source in the research brief. The About page
says so publicly.

## Social banner line (required for every story)

Each story needs one extra front-matter line, `hook`: a punchy line of 100 characters or fewer, written from the story itself with no new facts.
It is the banner on the Facebook picture and the Instagram cover, and the first line of the Instagram caption.
Example: "He won the World Cup on two rebuilt knees."

The Facebook post is the whole story. The tooling moves "Here's what I didn't know until recently." up so it begins within the first
230 characters, ahead of Facebook's "See more", and the remaining sentences of the opening follow it. Nothing is cut.
